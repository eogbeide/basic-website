    WHY FAMILY MEDICINE IS A PRIORITIZATION CLERKSHIP

    A single outpatient visit can contain an acute complaint, uncontrolled chronic disease, prevention gaps, medication problems and social barriers. The student must set an agenda, identify what is urgent, close high-value preventive gaps, avoid unnecessary testing and create a follow-up plan that works across time - not just solve one diagnosis.

    A. THE OUTPATIENT FIRST-PASS FRAME

    StepAsk yourselfOutput
    1. Agenda + acuityWhat brought the patient in? What is the patient most concerned about? Is there a red flag that changes the visit sequence?Shared agenda + urgent problems first.
    2. Context + medicationsAge, pregnancy possibility, comorbidities, allergies, adherence, OTC/supplement use, recent care, social access and health literacy?Accurate medication/reconciliation and context.
    3. Focused assessmentWhich history/exam findings materially change the differential or the need for testing?Problem representation + ranked outpatient differential.
    4. Chronic controlAre hypertension, diabetes, lipids, asthma/COPD, CKD, obesity or behavioral conditions controlled? Any complication or treatment toxicity?Control status + one or two highest-value actions.
    5. PreventionWhat screening, vaccination, counseling or reproductive/sexual-health needs are due based on age, risk and prior history?Current prevention plan tied to official guidance.
    6. Follow-up + safety netWhat should improve, when should the patient return, which result must be tracked, and what symptom should trigger urgent care?Closed loop: follow-up interval + contingency + ownership.

    B. PREVENTIVE CARE - THINK AGE + RISK + PRIOR HISTORY + PREFERENCE

    LaneHigh-yield reasoningShelf / clinic anchor
    Cancer screeningAge + organ/risk history + prior result + life expectancy + patient preference.Know common colorectal, cervical, breast and lung screening logic, but verify current USPSTF recommendations before using exact intervals.
    CardiometabolicBlood pressure, tobacco exposure, weight, diabetes risk, lipids/ASCVD risk, exercise and nutrition.Prevention questions often ask who should be screened/counseled and what finding requires confirmation before treatment.
    Vaccination / infectionAge, pregnancy, immune status, chronic disease, exposure/travel and prior immunization.Use the live CDC schedule and notes; do not rely on an old mnemonic for vaccine timing.
    Behavioral health / substanceDepression/anxiety, suicide risk when indicated, alcohol/drug use, sleep, intimate-partner violence and readiness to change.Primary care prevention includes behavioral health and brief intervention, not just cancer screening.
    Reproductive / sexual healthPregnancy intention, contraception, STI/HIV risk, menopause and preconception care when relevant.Preventive care is individualized by life stage and risk rather than assumed from age alone.
    Older adult functionFalls, cognition when indicated, hearing/vision, polypharmacy, function, goals of care and caregiver burden.A normal lab panel does not replace function, medication review and safety.
