    C. WHAT SCHOOL EXAMS COMMONLY REQUIRE

    ResourceDescriptionLink
    PK / dosingCalculate or infer bioavailability, Vd, clearance, half-life, steady state, loading/maintenance dose and first- vs zero-order elimination.
    PD / curvesRead log dose-response curves; distinguish potency from efficacy and full/partial agonists from competitive/noncompetitive antagonists.
    AutonomicsTranslate receptor + G protein/second messenger into organ effect, then predict agonist/antagonist indications and adverse effects.
    Metabolism / interactionsPhase I vs II, CYP induction/inhibition, prodrugs, renal/hepatic impairment and protein binding -> change in drug level or effect.
    Drug-class mechanismIdentify target/receptor/enzyme/channel and predict downstream physiology before using disease-name or medication-name recognition.
    Safety / toxicologyRecognize signature toxicity, monitoring and classic reversal/antidote principles; distinguish pharmacodynamic from pharmacokinetic interactions.

    D. 7-DAY SCHOOL-EXAM REVIEW CYCLE

    ResourceDescriptionLink
    Days 1-2PK/PD calculations + graphs. Rebuild equations and dose-response relationships from a blank page, then solve new problems.
    Days 3-4Autonomics + receptor signaling. Work organ-by-organ receptor predictions before attaching drugs to the map.
    Day 5Metabolism/interactions + antimicrobials. Practice CYP, clearance and resistance-mechanism vignettes.
    Day 6Cardio/renal + endocrine/CNS representative classes. Complete a timed mixed school-style block; classify every miss.
    Day 7Re-test misses on NEW curves/cases. Use one targeted video only if the mechanism remains unclear, then finish with a cumulative block.

    D. 7-DAY SCHOOL-EXAM REVIEW CYCLE — E. PHARMACOLOGY ERROR-LOG TAXONOMY

    • PK-equation gap - knew the formula but chose the wrong variable, units, direction or clinical interpretation.
    • Receptor/signaling gap - memorized the drug but not the receptor, second messenger or organ-level consequence.
    • MOA-to-effect gap - knew the target yet could not predict why it helps the disease or causes the adverse effect.
    • Interaction/metabolism gap - missed how CYP, renal/hepatic function, protein binding or another drug changes exposure/effect.
    • Class-confusion gap - mixed agents within a family because learning depended on names instead of the shared mechanism and discriminators.

    D. 7-DAY SCHOOL-EXAM REVIEW CYCLE — SCHOOL-EXAM GATE

    Proceed when you can solve unseen PK/PD graphs and dosing problems, infer drug effects from receptor/organ physiology, explain interactions mechanistically, and distinguish related drug classes using target + physiologic consequence rather than isolated memorization.
