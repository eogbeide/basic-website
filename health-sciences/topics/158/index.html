    D. GREEN / YELLOW / RED WITHOUT FAKE UNIVERSAL CUTOFFS

    StatusMeaningAction
    GREENMultiple independent signals agree: coverage complete, errors stable, timed performance sustainable, official assessment trend acceptable for the learner/school context, logistics ready.Maintain; taper broad new learning; rehearse format and protect recovery.
    YELLOWMixed evidence: one or two noncritical gates lag, trend is uncertain, or assessment conditions were not representative.Repair the specific gate; repeat an appropriate readiness check under better conditions.
    REDCritical unresolved issue: major content blind spot, repeated high-frequency error cluster, severe timing/endurance problem, contradictory official assessments, or authorization/logistics barrier.Do not average it away. Escalate to school/faculty guidance and create a targeted repair plan.

    D. GREEN / YELLOW / RED WITHOUT FAKE UNIVERSAL CUTOFFS — CRITICAL-GATE RULE

    Do not compute a homemade readiness average. Some gates are bottlenecks: unresolved authorization, a major blueprint blind spot, repeated unsafe reasoning, or severe time collapse can matter more than a strong average elsewhere.

    E. MINIMUM DATA PACKET FOR A REAL READINESS DECISION

    • Blueprint map: what has / has not been learned and tested.
    • Qbank analytics: recent mixed performance, low-confidence corrects, repeat-error clusters and pacing.
    • Official / school assessment trend: at least the most recent relevant forms, with timing mode and date documented.
    • Endurance sample: representative timed work long enough to reveal late-block decline.
    • Logistics checklist: authorization, permit/scheduling, site/ID, accommodations, software/interface, breaks and arrival plan.
    • School guidance: required thresholds and timing decisions when your program specifies them.

    E. MINIMUM DATA PACKET FOR A REAL READINESS DECISION — WHY THIS PREVENTS FALSE CONFIDENCE

    A learner can look strong in one dimension and still be fragile overall: high Qbank scores with repeated questions; high self-paced scores with weak timing; broad coverage with poor retrieval; or good knowledge with unresolved test-day logistics.
