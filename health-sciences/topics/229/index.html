    A. READINESS-SIGNAL DASHBOARD — 1. Readiness is convergent evidence, not one number DEEP EXPLANATION One assessment is a sample of performance on one day. Confidence rises when recent official-style assessment trends, mixed-question transfer, weak-domain stability, pacing and execution point in the same

    direction.

    2. A readiness estimate is not the same as a learning diagnosis DEEP EXPLANATION NBME can help gauge overall readiness and show content-area performance, but remediation still requires asking what failed: knowledge, mechanism, discrimination, representation, timing or endurance. WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Students often respond to a low result with more of the same study activity instead of identifying the failure mode. EASIEST CLUE Score says where risk exists; error analysis says what to do next. — 3. Stability matters more than one peak DEEP EXPLANATION A strong result is more meaningful when nearby practice and assessments are compatible with it. High variance demands investigation of content mix, fatigue, pacing, testing conditions and weak-domain

    recurrence.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT The best score is emotionally attractive and can become the planning anchor even when it is an outlier. EASIEST CLUE Trust the trend and the process before the single best day. — B. CURRENT OFFICIAL ANCHORS

    • NBME describes CBSSA as a tool to establish a baseline, track longitudinal trends, identify content-area strengths/weaknesses and gauge Step 1 readiness.
    • For Step 1 exams on/after May 14, 2026: fourteen 30-minute blocks in an 8-hour session, with no more than 20 items per block; use the current official interactive testing experience before test day.
