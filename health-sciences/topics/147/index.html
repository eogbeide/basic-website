    Many high-performing students lose points not because they forgot a disease, but because they choose the right idea at the wrong time. This page deliberately trains the domains that cut across every organ system.

    G. MANAGEMENT-SEQUENCE PATTERNS — H. ETHICS / COMMUNICATION / PATIENT SAFETY

    • Capacity: decision-specific and time-specific; distinguish capacity from legal competence. Address reversible causes and use surrogate decision-making when appropriate.
    • Informed consent: explain material risks, benefits, alternatives and the option of no treatment; emergencies and lack of capacity change the pathway.
    • Confidentiality: protect patient information but recognize legally/clinically required exceptions; adolescent and public-health contexts can modify the rule.
    • Error / systems questions: disclose clinically meaningful errors appropriately, stabilize the patient, report through the safety system, and improve the process rather than conceal or blame.
    • Communication: use qualified interpreters for clinical communication; respond to emotion before returning to information; clarify goals in serious illness.

    Exam skillWhat to retrieve

    Treatment effectARR, RRR, NNT/NNH; distinguish statistical from clinical significance.
    Study design / biasRandomization, blinding, confounding, selection, information bias, intention-to-treat and loss to follow-up.

    J. 2026 NUTRITION EMPHASIS

    • Step 2 now lists Nutrition at 15-20% discipline overlap. Do not treat nutrition as a small biochemistry appendix.
    • Retrieve malnutrition risk, obesity counseling, micronutrient deficiency, pregnancy/pediatric nutrition, enteral vs parenteral support, refeeding risk, alcohol-related deficiency, bariatric consequences and disease-specific diet counseling.
    • Nutrition questions usually present as clinical management or prevention, not “name the vitamin” recall.
