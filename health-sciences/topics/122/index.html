    PRINCIPLE — THE CORE EXAM TRAP

    Two answer choices may both describe real psychiatric symptoms. The discriminator is often duration, relationship to mood episodes, functional decline, substance/medication exposure, fluctuating attention, or whether psychosis persists outside mood symptoms. Build a timeline before naming the disorder.

    C. HIGH-YIELD SYNDROME DIFFERENTIATION

    ClusterDifferentiate byClerkship / exam anchor
    Depressive disordersEpisode duration, anhedonia/depressed mood, neurovegetative symptoms, impairment, bereavement context, psychotic features, bipolar history.Always screen for mania/hypomania before treating apparent unipolar depression; assess suicide risk and medical/substance mimics.
    Bipolar spectrumPast/current mania or hypomania, decreased need for sleep, increased goal-directed activity, grandiosity, pressured speech, risky behavior; impairment/psychosis separates severity.Acute mania changes disposition and medication choices; antidepressant monotherapy can be unsafe in bipolar illness.
    Psychotic disordersDuration + decline + mood relationship: brief psychosis, schizophreniform, schizophrenia, schizoaffective, mood disorder with psychotic features.Ask whether psychosis occurs in the absence of a major mood episode; exclude substances, delirium and neurologic/medical causes.
    Anxiety / OCD / traumaTrigger, intrusive thoughts vs generalized worry vs panic vs trauma re-experiencing/avoidance; compulsions; duration and impairment.Differentiate obsession from delusion and panic from cardiopulmonary/endocrine causes when clinically indicated.
    Substance-related disordersIntoxication vs withdrawal vs substance-induced mood/psychosis vs primary disorder; timing around last use; autonomic findings.Know dangerous withdrawals (alcohol/benzodiazepines) and opioid/stimulant syndromes; treat immediate physiologic risk first.
    NeurocognitiveDelirium = acute/fluctuating attention; dementia = chronic cognitive decline; depression may mimic cognitive impairment.Inattention + fluctuation + medical illness/medication exposure is delirium until proven otherwise.
    Eating disordersRestriction/binge/purge patterns, body-image disturbance, weight trajectory, vital/lab/ECG consequences.Medical stability precedes psychotherapy framing; recognize refeeding risk and arrhythmia/electrolyte danger.
    Personality / somatic / factitiousPervasive long-term pattern vs episodic illness; symptom focus, intentionality, external incentives and functional impact.Do not confuse personality traits with acute mood/psychotic episodes; maintain nonjudgmental, boundary-based care.
    Neurodevelopmental / childDevelopmental history, school/home impairment, communication/social patterns, attention/impulsivity and age of onset.Symptoms must fit developmental context and occur across appropriate settings; consider learning, sleep, trauma and substance factors.

    C. HIGH-YIELD SYNDROME DIFFERENTIATION — D. PSYCHOSIS + MOOD TIMELINE CHECK • Psychosis + mood: determine whether psychotic symptoms occur only during mood episodes or also for a sustained period without them. • First episode: obtain collateral and review substances/medications plus neurologic/medical red flags before assuming a primary psychotic disorder. • Depression: screen for past mania/hypomania, psychosis, substance use and suicidality; treatment depends on the longitudinal diagnosis. • Delirium: attention and fluctuation outrank hallucination content; a medically ill patient with new psychosis may need medical rather than psychiatric admission.
