    YEAR 2 | PAGE 51A - 1 NORMAL -> 2 FAILURE -> 3 MANIFESTATION: BUILD THE MODEL BEFORE THE DIAGNOSIS

    THE INTEGRATION RULE: ONE DISEASE MODEL, MANY REPRESENTATIONS. START WITH NORMAL ANATOMY / PHYSIOLOGY / HISTOLOGY, IDENTIFY THE FAILED MECHANISM, THEN PREDICT THE SYMPTOMS, SIGNS, LABS, GRAPHS, IMAGES AND PATHOLOGY THAT MUST FOLLOW. ONLY THEN MOVE TO DIFFERENTIAL, TESTS AND THERAPY. A. LINKS 1-3 - THE CAUSAL MODEL

    LANECORE QUESTIONWHAT MASTERY LOOKS LIKE
    1. NORMALWhat is the normal structure, flow, regulation, signaling or tissue function?Can reconstruct the normal model without notes and state the variables that should change when it is perturbed.
    2. FAILUREWhat exactly broke: obstruction, destruction, dysregulation, ischemia, infection, autoimmunity, neoplasia, toxicity or signaling failure?Names the failed process and places it upstream of the memorized disease label.
    3. MANIFESTATIONWhat findings must follow from that failure across symptoms, exam, labs, physiology, imaging and pathology?Predicts direction-of-change and links every major clue back to the mechanism instead of collecting unrelated associations.
    Representation transferCan the same mechanism be recognized as a graph, image, microscopic finding, lab pattern or vignette?Moves across representations without relearning the concept as separate facts.

    B. TOP 10 MOST-MISSED INTEGRATION CONCEPTS - #1 TO #3

    # / CONCEPTDEEP EXPLANATIONWHY STUDENTS MISS ITEASIEST CLUE
    1. Normal first is not optionalPathology is a deviation from a normal model. If normal pressure-flow, feedback, transport, anatomy or tissue architecture is unstable, disease facts become disconnected memorization.Learners recognize disease names faster than they can explain the normal variable that changed.Before any disease, state what normal function is being lost or distorted.
    2. Mechanism must sit upstream of the findingsA syndrome becomes predictable when the causal chain is explicit: insult -> failed process -> physiologic consequence -> clinical manifestation. The same chain should explain multiple clues.Lists of symptoms and buzzwords feel efficient but do not transfer to unfamiliar cases.Ask what one failure explains the greatest number of findings?
    3. Prediction is stronger than recognitionMastery means predicting what should increase, decrease, appear or disappear before seeing the answer choices. This exposes weak causal understanding and prepares for graphs/images/labs.Answer choices can cue recognition and hide fragile knowledge.Cover the options and predict direction + finding first.
