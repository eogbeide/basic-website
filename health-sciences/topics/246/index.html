    C. REPEATABLE CLERKSHIP CADENCE — 4. Clinical exposure is powerful but systematically incomplete DEEP EXPLANATION A rotation exposes you to the local census, referral patterns, season and service. The subject exam samples the discipline more broadly. Patient-linked learning creates depth; the blueprint and Qbank deliberately fill the

    presentations your service never showed you.

    C. REPEATABLE CLERKSHIP CADENCE — WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Real patients feel more important and memorable than unseen blueprint topics, so gaps remain invisible until the exam. EASIEST CLUE Keep an unseen-but-testable list and intentionally review at least one missing presentation each week. 5. Question review should reconstruct the decision sequence DEEP EXPLANATION For each important miss, rebuild the chain: problem representation -> differential -> instability/must-not-miss -> next test -> initial management -> definitive management -> disposition/follow-up. This converts an answer

    explanation into clinical reasoning.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT It is faster to memorize the fact that made the option correct than to rebuild the sequence that makes the fact useful. EASIEST CLUE If you know the diagnosis but repeatedly miss the next step, your gap is probably sequencing, not disease recognition. 6. Oral presentations and notes are reasoning tools, not clerical products DEEP EXPLANATION A strong presentation or assessment/plan reveals whether you can prioritize data, rank a differential, express uncertainty, justify tests and commit to management. Concise communication is therefore part of diagnostic

    mastery, not separate from it.
