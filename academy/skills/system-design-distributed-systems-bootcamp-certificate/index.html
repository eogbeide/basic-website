Bootcamp Mastery Certificate B20

Primary spine: MIT 6.824 Distributed Systems, reinforced with Gaurav Sen system-design application. Build first-principles fluency in RPC, distributed storage, replication, consensus, coordination and transactions, then translate those primitives into scalable service architecture and explicit reliability tradeoffs.

Benchmark: MIT 6.824 and Gaurav Sen publish the linked videos publicly. Zuyini curates them into an independent hands-on mastery path and requires original architecture, implementation/simulation and failure evidence; completion does not imply an MIT- or creator-issued certificate or endorsement.

Build-Along Checkpoints

  • Checkpoint 1: Write a system brief with functional/nonfunctional requirements, scale assumptions, latency/availability targets and failure budget.
  • Checkpoint 2: Prototype an RPC-based service pair; add timeouts, retries and idempotency, then demonstrate at least one concurrency/failure bug and fix.
  • Checkpoint 3: Design replicated state using primary/backup or quorum-style reasoning; document consistency, recovery and stale-read tradeoffs.
  • Checkpoint 4: Implement or simulate a small Raft-style consensus workflow and show leader election, log agreement and behavior under node failure.
  • Checkpoint 5: Add coordination/transactions for one cross-service workflow; reason about locking, atomicity, partial failure and exactly-once illusions.
  • Checkpoint 6: Produce capacity estimates and an architecture with load balancing, partitioning, caching/queues and observability; run a failure drill.

7-Day Independent Capstone

  • Day 1: Choose a realistic service and define requests/sec, data volume, latency SLO, availability, consistency needs and top failure modes.
  • Day 2: Build a small RPC/service prototype; instrument calls and add timeouts, retries, idempotency keys and backpressure assumptions.
  • Day 3: Add replicated state and a failure simulation; explain consistency, failover, recovery and what clients observe during disruption.
  • Day 4: Implement or simulate leader election/log replication; inject node/network faults and capture consensus/recovery evidence.
  • Day 5: Design one multi-service transaction/coordination flow and compare at least two approaches for atomicity and failure handling.
  • Day 6: Complete capacity model and production architecture; add caching/queue/partitioning where justified plus traces, metrics and SLOs.
  • Day 7: Publish repo/simulation, architecture diagrams, ADRs, capacity math, failure report, tradeoff memo, demo video and operations runbook.

Distributed Systems - Design, Replicate, Coordinate & Recover

Design and prove a distributed system whose architecture follows measurable requirements rather than interview buzzwords. Completion must show concurrency/RPC behavior, replication and consensus reasoning, coordination/transaction tradeoffs, scale estimates, observability and evidence from injected failures.

Proof Rubric — What Must Be Shown

  • REQUIREMENTS: Scale + SLOs + failure budget
  • RPC: Timeouts, retries + idempotency
  • REPLICATION: Consistency + recovery evidence
  • CONSENSUS: Leader/log behavior under faults
  • COORDINATION: Transactions + partial-failure reasoning
  • ARCHITECTURE: Scale patterns tied to constraints
  • OBSERVABILITY: Metrics/traces + failure drill
  • PORTFOLIO: ADRs, diagrams, demo + runbook
