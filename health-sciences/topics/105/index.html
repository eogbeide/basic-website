    YEAR 3 | PAGE 29A - WARD WORKFLOW + CLINICAL REASONING

    WHY THIS CLERKSHIP MATTERS

    Internal Medicine is the broadest adult-medicine clerkship. The goal is not just to recognize diagnoses; it is to build a prioritized problem representation, choose the next diagnostic step, begin safe initial management, reassess response, and communicate the plan clearly on rounds.

    A. DAILY PATIENT WORKFLOW

    StageWhat the student should doOutput
    Pre-roundReview overnight events, vitals, I/O, key labs/imaging, medication changes and active safety issues.One-sentence update + anticipated problems.
    BedsideFocused interval history/exam tied to the active problems; confirm symptoms rather than copying yesterday.New data that changes assessment or plan.
    ReasoningUpdate problem representation; rank differential by likelihood + danger; decide what evidence would change management.Prioritized A/P, not a list of facts.
    RoundsPresent concisely: illness script -> overnight -> focused data -> assessment -> next step.Defensible plan and explicit uncertainty.
    After roundsExecute supervised tasks, follow results, read around 1-3 patients and close loops before sign-out.Action completed + contingency communicated.

    A. DAILY PATIENT WORKFLOW — B. CLINICAL REASONING FRAME

    • Problem representation = age/context + tempo + dominant syndrome + key discriminators.
    • Differential = common + dangerous + unifying; rank it and state why the leading diagnosis leads.
    • Testing = ask what result will change probability, treatment, disposition or urgency.
    • Management = stabilize first when needed; then disease-specific therapy, prevention, medication safety and follow-up.
    • Reassessment = response to treatment is new diagnostic data; revise the plan when the patient does not behave as expected.
