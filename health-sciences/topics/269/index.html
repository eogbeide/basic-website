MORE RESOURCES DO NOT AUTOMATICALLY PRODUCE MORE TRANSFER. A. SOURCE-DERIVED RESOURCE ARCHITECTURE - PRESERVED + OPERATIONALIZED — 1. A resource stack needs defined roles - not several resources doing the same job DEEP EXPLANATION The source page already separates clinical framework, primary Qbank, targeted reference, case reasoning, bedside skills and official readiness. The upgrade makes that

    separation explicit: each layer answers a different learning problem. Redundant resources should be added only when a defined gap persists. WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Students often interpret a long resource list as a checklist to complete. They watch two full video courses, read two references and run multiple Qbanks even when the bottleneck is a single reasoning or management skill.

If there is no distinct job, do not add it. 2. Passive content should repair a named gap, then hand the learner back to retrieval and questions DEEP EXPLANATION Videos and references are useful when a clinical framework is missing, but Step 2 transfer is demonstrated by generating the pathway without prompts and applying it to a new

    vignette. A repair is therefore incomplete until active retrieval and a new question confirm it.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Passive review feels fluent and can temporarily make difficult material feel familiar, so learners remain in videos/notes longer than necessary. EASIEST CLUE Use watch/read -> close the source -> reconstruct -> new question. If you cannot reconstruct, the content pass was not finished. 3. A stop rule protects time from low-yield depth and resource hopping DEEP EXPLANATION A stop rule is the predeclared condition for leaving a resource: the uncertainty is resolved, the algorithm can be reproduced, or new-question performance confirms transfer.

    Without it, every explanation can expand into an unlimited review tree.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Clinical resources are interconnected; one missed detail can lead to ten tabs, hours of reading and very little additional exam-relevant discrimination. EASIEST CLUE Before opening a resource, write the one question you need answered and the evidence that will let you close it.
