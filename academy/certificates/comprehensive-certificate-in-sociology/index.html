Integrative Capstone

Formulate a sociological question about an organization, market, community or policy. Build a theory and literature map; operationalize the core constructs; collect or curate qualitative and quantitative evidence; document sampling and ethics; code qualitative data transparently and analyze quantitative patterns; confront causal limits and alternative mechanisms; compare groups, institutions or time periods; then produce a research memo plus a public-facing data story or organizational recommendation.

Evidence Standard

  • Theory-evidence alignment
  • construct validity
  • ethical sampling
  • transparent qualitative coding
  • reproducible quantitative analysis
  • careful subgroup definitions
  • causal humility
  • explicit alternative mechanisms
  • context-aware interpretation of inequality and institutions
  • mixed-method triangulation
  • and clear communication without unsupported generalization. RELEASE QUALITY STANDARD: long-form academic instruction, reproducible evidence, explicit limitations and a capstone artifact that can be inspected - not just claimed
