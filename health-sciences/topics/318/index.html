    B. 10 HIGH-FREQUENCY COGNITIVE TRAPS

    TrapWhat it looks likeCountermove
    AnchoringInitial impression remains dominant despite later discordant data.Restate the case using only current evidence; ask what finding the anchor cannot explain.
    Premature closure Confirmation biasThe search stops once one plausible diagnosis is found. You seek/support data for the favored diagnosis and discount contradiction.Require a dangerous alternative and one competing explanation before closure. Actively search for disconfirming evidence and the strongest rival hypothesis.
    Availability RepresentativenessA recent or memorable case makes a diagnosis feel more likely than it is. Atypical features make a real diagnosis feel impossible because it does not match the prototype.Reset to prevalence + patient-specific discriminators. Use criteria, mechanism and probability; do not demand textbook appearance.
    Search satisficingFinding one abnormality ends the search even when the whole presentation is unexplained.Ask whether the finding explains every major feature; continue if residual discordance remains.
    Framing effectPrior labels, demographics, handoff wording or emotionally charged information shape the conclusion.Rebuild the problem representation from raw data before accepting the frame.
    Base-rate neglect OverconfidenceRare diagnoses are overweighted because their pattern seems exciting or elegant. Confidence exceeds evidence; uncertainty and alternatives are not made explicit.Start with prior probability, then update with likelihood-changing evidence. State confidence level, unresolved evidence and what would trigger reconsideration.
    Diagnostic momentum / blind obedienceA diagnosis or test interpretation is inherited because others already accepted it.Independently reconstruct the case; verify whether the evidence still supports the label.
