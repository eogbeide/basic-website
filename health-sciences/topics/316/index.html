    F. 10 HIGH-DISCRIMINATION SCHEDULING DECISIONS

    #PatternBest next decision
    1The weekly plan exceeds the hours that remain after mandatory duties and recovery.Cut scope before cutting sleep; preserve coverage floor, primary practice and top repair targets.
    2 3A mandatory class/clinical/work task removes a planned study block. A strong system still has several hours of broad review scheduled.Re-rank the queue; move one high-value task and let low-priority passive work drop. Convert it to brief maintenance and redirect deep time to recurring weak domains.
    4 5After a long clinical/school day, concentration is poor. The same error returns after two prior reviews.Run the minimum viable study day instead of forcing a dedicated-style marathon. Give it a protected high-focus repair window; change the repair method and schedule delayed proof.
    6Two days of the original schedule are now behind.Discard the outdated calendar and rebuild from current evidence; do not double every remaining day.
    7 8Late-night studying is followed by worse next-day retrieval or timing. Exam is close and a new comprehensive resource looks attractive.Move the stop time earlier and cut low-output work; protect the next performance cycle. Do not start it unless one precise unresolved gap justifies the switch and there is time to verify transfer.
    9A dual-role learner has work/family duties that vary unpredictably.Anchor fixed commitments + recovery; use movable repair blocks and a defined minimum viable day.
    10Every hour is scheduled and there is no contingency space.Create buffer by dropping duplication; robustness is part of the plan, not wasted time.
