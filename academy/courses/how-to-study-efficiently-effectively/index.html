    How to Study Efficiently & Effectively

    Build an evidence-based study system that improves retention, transfer and exam performance without simply adding more hours. Learn metacognition, focus, deep processing, retrieval practice, spacing, interleaving, self-regulated learning, deliberate practice and feedback.

    BASIC
    • ▶Beliefs That Make You Fail...Or Succeed — Stephen Chew / Samford (7-8 min)
      Correct common learning myths, calibrate metacognition and understand why distraction, shallow study and overconfidence waste time.
    • ▶Study Strategies: Retrieval Practice — The Learning Scientists (Short)
      Replace passive rereading with active recall, self-testing and producing knowledge from memory.
    • ▶Study Strategies: Spaced Practice — The Learning Scientists (Short)
      Turn cramming into a distributed study schedule that revisits material across days and weeks.
    INTERMEDIATE
    • ▶What Students Should Understand About How People Learn — Stephen Chew
      Understand deep versus shallow processing and why what you think about while studying matters more than intention alone.
    • ▶Cognitive Principles for Optimizing Learning — Stephen Chew
      Use elaboration, distinctiveness, personal meaning and appropriate retrieval/application to create deeper learning.
    • ▶Putting the Principles for Optimizing Learning into Practice — Stephen Chew
      Transform reading, highlighting and note-taking from passive exposure into active, meaning-focused study.
    • ▶Study Strategies: Interleaving — Video The Learning Scientists
      Why this level Mix related problem types or concepts so you practice choosing the right method, not merely repeating one pattern.
    • ▶Self-Regulated Learning — Stephen Chew / Samford
      Plan, monitor and adapt your learning strategy based on goals, task demands, progress and feedback.
    • ▶I Blew the Exam, Now What? — Stephen Chew
      Convert poor results into diagnostic evidence: identify knowledge, strategy and execution errors, then change the next study cycle.
