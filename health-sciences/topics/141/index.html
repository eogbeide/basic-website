    YEAR 4 | PAGE 34A - OWN THE PATIENT, NOT THE SUPERVISION

    THE YEAR 4 LEAP Move from “I know the diagnosis” to “I can run a safe clinical day”: anticipate needs, prioritize work, draft orders and plans for review, close loops, reassess, communicate uncertainty and escalate early. Autonomy should increase only within the supervision level granted by your school and clinical team.

    A. THE INTERN-CYCLE - REPEAT IT FOR EVERY PATIENT

    StageStudent executionVisible output
    Pre-roundReview overnight events, vitals, I/O, new labs/imaging, medications, procedures, consult recommendations and pending tasks.One-line update + what changed + what could become unsafe today.
    BedsideFocused interval history/exam tied to active problems; verify symptoms, devices, oxygen, pain, mobility and discharge barriers.New bedside data that changes the assessment or plan.
    SynthesizeRebuild the problem list by priority. For each problem: status -> goal -> evidence -> action -> next decision -> contingency.Problem-oriented A/P that can be defended rather than copied forward.
    RoundsPresent concisely; state uncertainty; propose the next diagnostic/management step and the reason it is next.Supervisor can accept, modify or reject a clear plan.
    ExecuteComplete assigned tasks, draft orders/prescriptions for review, call consults, arrange studies, coordinate with nursing/pharmacy/social work.Closed-loop tasks, not a growing checklist.
    ReassessTrack response to therapy and new results; update the plan when the patient does not behave as predicted.“Result -> meaning -> action -> communication.”
    Handoff / cross-coverIdentify sickest patients, active threats, pending items and explicit “if/then” contingencies.Receiver knows what matters tonight and when to escalate.

    B. THE FIVE-LINE PATIENT CONTROL PANEL

    • 1. Problem representation: who is this patient, what is the dominant syndrome, and what changed today?
    • 2. Instability check: airway/breathing/circulation/neurologic status, trend, and what would trigger immediate escalation.
    • 3. Decision point: what single result or clinical change is most likely to alter management next?
    • 4. Disposition trajectory: what must happen before discharge, transfer, procedure, ICU escalation or continued observation?
    • 5. Contingency: if the expected course fails, what is the next action and who must be called?

    B. THE FIVE-LINE PATIENT CONTROL PANEL — C. PATIENT -> LEARNING TRANSFER

    For one or two patients each day, convert the encounter into a reusable illness script: presentation + dangerous alternative + next test + initial treatment + disposition + one complication/safety issue. Keep the learning product short enough to retrieve on the next rotation.
