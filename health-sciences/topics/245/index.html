    A. PATIENT-TO-EXAM CONVERSION CHAIN — 1. Patient exposure becomes learning only after abstraction DEEP EXPLANATION A memorable patient is one example, not the disease model. Converting the encounter into an illness script separates generalizable features from incidental details and makes the knowledge reusable on a different

    patient and on a Shelf/COMAT vignette.

    A. PATIENT-TO-EXAM CONVERSION CHAIN — WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Clinical encounters feel vivid, so familiarity with one case is easily mistaken for transferable mastery. EASIEST CLUE After the encounter, state who gets it -> how it presents -> what mechanism explains it -> what changes management without looking at the chart. 2. A problem representation compresses; it does not transcribe DEEP EXPLANATION The one-liner should preserve the features that change probability: age/risk, tempo, syndrome, severity, location, physiology and decisive positives/negatives. Details that do not change the differential should usually

    disappear from the compressed representation.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Students equate completeness with quality and repeat the entire HPI instead of prioritizing information. EASIEST CLUE Ask: if I could keep only 4-6 descriptors, which ones would change my differential or next step? 3. Stability and must-not-miss alternatives can outrank diagnostic elegance DEEP EXPLANATION Clinical reasoning is sequenced. An unstable patient may need ABCs, resuscitation, source control or another urgent action before a definitive diagnosis. Even in stable patients, dangerous alternatives may need

    exclusion before the most likely benign diagnosis is accepted.
