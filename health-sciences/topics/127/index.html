    FOLLOW-UP — CONTINUITY CHANGES THE QUESTION

    Family medicine repeatedly asks what to do after the diagnosis is already known. The answer may be to confirm control, detect complications, simplify medications, improve adherence, counsel behavior change, address cost/access, coordinate referrals or choose the correct follow-up interval. A good plan is measurable and sustainable.

    E. CHRONIC-DISEASE CONTROL - USE THE SAME FIVE QUESTIONS

    ConditionControl / complication questionsManagement logic
    HypertensionWas the diagnosis confirmed appropriately? Current BP pattern, adherence, secondary causes, renal/CV disease, medication effects?Separate routine chronic control from hypertensive emergency; choose therapy around comorbidity, pregnancy potential, renal function and adverse effects.
    DiabetesA1c/glucose trend, hypoglycemia, kidney/eye/neuropathy risk, BP/lipids, medications, nutrition/activity and access.Management is more than glucose: reduce cardiovascular/renal risk, screen complications and tailor therapy to patient factors.
    Lipids / ASCVD riskKnown ASCVD, LDL pattern, diabetes, age/risk, smoking/BP, statin tolerance and adherence.Know primary vs secondary prevention logic and when risk/conditions change treatment intensity.
    Asthma / COPDSymptom frequency, exacerbations, inhaler technique/adherence, smoking/exposure, spirometry context, steroid burden.Distinguish baseline controller adjustment from acute exacerbation treatment; technique and trigger reduction matter.
    CKDeGFR/albuminuria trend, BP/diabetes control, medication dosing, potassium/volume status, nephrotoxin exposure.Protect kidney function, dose safely and recognize when progression/complications justify nephrology input.
    Obesity / metabolic healthTrajectory, sleep, medications, nutrition/activity, complications, readiness, barriers and patient goals.Use non-stigmatizing, longitudinal care; combine behavioral, pharmacologic or procedural strategies when indicated rather than one-off advice.
    Depression / anxiety / substance useSeverity, function, suicide risk when indicated, response/adverse effects, adherence, therapy access, substance pattern.Primary care owns longitudinal behavioral health: safety first, then evidence-based treatment, follow-up and escalation when needed.
