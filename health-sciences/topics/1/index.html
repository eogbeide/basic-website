    THE MASTERY LOOP

    For each course or clerkship: Learn -> Retrieve -> Apply -> Test -> Remediate -> Re-test. Passive watching alone is not sufficient. Every block should end with question-based retrieval and an assessment tied to the relevant school or licensing-exam blueprint.

    Recommended study architecture

    LayerGoalTypical tools
    1. Concept buildUnderstand mechanisms and mental models.School lectures + substantive videos + core text/notes.
    2. Active recallMake key facts and relationships retrievable.Spaced repetition / flashcards / closed-book recall.
    3. Question practiceTranslate knowledge into vignette reasoning.UWorld, AMBOSS, TrueLearn/COMBANK, school question banks.
    4. Clinical applicationUse knowledge in cases, physical exam and management decisions.Standardized patients, simulation, bedside teaching, OnlineMedEd, Stanford Medicine 25.
    5. Readiness checkUse external assessments before high-stakes exams.NBME CBSSA/CCSSA/CSMS; USMLE sample interface; NBOME/COMAT-SE.
    6. RemediationTurn misses into a targeted plan.Error log -> targeted video/article -> new questions -> timed reassessment.

    Recommended study architecture — Rules for this Academy

    • Official blueprints are the source of truth. Exam formats and content can change; always verify on USMLE/NBME/NBOME before test day.
    • School exams come first during the block. Map each school objective to the Academy module, then add board-style questions on the same topic.
    • Questions are learning tools. Review why every option is right or wrong, not just the correct answer.
    • Videos are selected for depth. Use long-form teaching to build mechanisms; use short clips only when they efficiently demonstrate a focused exam or procedure.
    • Clinical competence requires supervised practice. No video curriculum substitutes for anatomy lab, standardized patients, simulation, clerkships or direct patient care.
