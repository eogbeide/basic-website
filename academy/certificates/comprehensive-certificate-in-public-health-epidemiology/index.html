Comprehensive Certificate in Public Health & Epidemiology

A university- and agency-benchmarked certificate spanning population health, epidemiologic study design, biostatistics, surveillance, outbreak investigation, health equity, environmental/occupational health, causal inference and evidence-to-policy translation.

University Curriculum Benchmark: Harvard Epidemiology & Biostatistics | Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School | CDC Field Epidemiology Manual

FOUNDATIONS / INTRODUCTION

  • Public health systems, prevention, determinants of health + health equity
  • Incidence/prevalence, risk/rate/odds, bias/confounding + study design
  • Biostatistics, uncertainty, effect measures + reproducible R/Python workflows

★ Recommended after FOUNDATIONS / INTRODUCTION — every lesson below is still open.

INTERMEDIATE CORE

  • Disease surveillance, case definitions, epi curves, time-place-person
  • Outbreak investigation, lab collaboration, environmental/occupational/global health
  • Program evaluation, screening, diagnostic performance + evidence appraisal

★ Recommended after INTERMEDIATE CORE — every lesson below is still open.

ADVANCED / SPECIALIZATION

  • Causal inference, quasi-experiments + real-world observational evidence
  • Spatial/temporal analytics, dashboards + risk communication
  • Intervention design, implementation science, policy analysis + equity impact

Direct Learning Bridges

  • ▶Public Health learning resources / MOOCs — Johns Hopkins
  • ▶Field Epidemiology Manual chapters — CDC
  • ▶Introduction to Epidemiology & Biostatistics — Harvard
  • ▶Conducting a Field Investigation — CDC

Integrative Capstone

01: Define a population-health question, causal model and measurable outcome; write a brief protocol with target population and bias/confounding plan. 02: Acquire or simulate a de-identified dataset; create a reproducible R/Python analysis with descriptive epidemiology, effect estimates, uncertainty and sensitivity checks. 03: Construct a surveillance/outbreak scenario with case definition, line list, epidemic curve, hypothesis generation and a control-measure decision. 04: Evaluate one intervention or policy using an appropriate experimental, quasi-experimental or observational design; explicitly discuss equity and unintended effects. 05: Publish a dashboard or evidence figure set plus an executive/public-health brief that separates evidence, assumptions, uncertainty and recommendations.

Evidence Standard

  • A defensible epidemiologic question
  • correct study design and measures
  • reproducible data lineage
  • explicit bias/confounding analysis
  • sensible causal claims
  • credible outbreak/surveillance logic
  • equity and ethics
  • clear risk communication
  • and a decision-ready intervention/policy recommendation with limitations
