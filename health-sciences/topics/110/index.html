    C. POSTOPERATIVE DETERIORATION - THINK BY SYNDROME, NOT BY MNEMONIC

    PresentationFirst-pass reasoningHigh-yield causes / decisions
    Tachycardia / hypotensionIs this hemorrhage, hypovolemia, sepsis, cardiopulmonary stress or pain?Reassess perfusion, bleeding, medications, drains and source; stabilize while defining the cause.
    Hypoxemia / dyspneaAirway/ventilation problem, aspiration/pneumonia, atelectatic change, PE, pulmonary edema, pneumothorax?Use severity + bedside findings to choose oxygen/ventilatory support and targeted imaging/testing.
    Fever / infection concernLocalizing symptoms/signs and host/procedure risk matter more than the postoperative day number.Consider wound/organ-space infection, pneumonia, urinary/device infection, line infection and noninfectious causes.
    Oliguria / AKIIs renal perfusion low, the urinary tract obstructed, or intrinsic injury developing?Check volume/perfusion context, medications, catheter patency and renal labs; avoid reflex fluid without reassessment.
    Nausea / distention / no bowel functionExpected postoperative ileus vs medication/electrolyte effect vs mechanical obstruction or complication?Review operation, exam, bowel pattern and imaging need; recognize peritonitis, ischemia or leak as escalation signals.
    Wound / drain abnormalityHematoma, seroma, dehiscence, SSI, abscess, fistula or anastomotic leak?Look for systemic toxicity and source-control need; antibiotics alone do not substitute for drainage/debridement when indicated.
    Mental-status changeMedication, hypoxemia, infection, metabolic disturbance, withdrawal, stroke or delirium?Correct reversible threats first and do not attribute acute change to "just postoperative" status.

    D. FLUIDS, ELECTROLYTES + NUTRITION - THREE DIFFERENT JOBS

    Fluids / electrolytesNutrition
    Resuscitation: restore perfusion in shock or major volume loss; reassess after each intervention rather than giving fluid by habit.Use the gut when feasible: oral/enteral nutrition usually preserves gut function and should be distinguished from parenteral nutrition.
    Maintenance: replace normal daily water/electrolyte needs when oral intake is inadequate.Recognize high-risk states: malnutrition, prolonged poor intake, major catabolic illness, GI discontinuity/obstruction and refeeding risk.
    Replacement: match ongoing losses from vomiting, NG suction, drains, fistula, diarrhea or high-output ostomy.Monitor: tolerance, glucose, electrolytes, fluid balance and complications of access/feeding route.
    Recheck: I/O, weight, exam, urine output, renal function, sodium/potassium/magnesium/phosphate and acid-base context.Shelf logic: choose the route and timing that fit GI function, aspiration risk and the patient's operative course.
