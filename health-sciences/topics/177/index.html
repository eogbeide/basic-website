    A physiologic disturbance causes a primary change; homeostatic systems then respond in the opposite direction when possible. Correct reasoning identifies what changed first, what compensates, and what cannot fully normalize.

TOP 10 MOST-MISSED FOUNDATIONAL SCIENCES II CONCEPTS - #1 TO #3 1. Primary perturbation and compensation must be separated Deep explanation

    Easiest clueAsk: what moved first? Then label every other change as direct consequence, compensation or unrelated.
    2. Graphs and equations are alternate representations of the same relationship Deep explanationPV loops, flow curves, spirometry, renal clearance and dose-response-like physiologic graphs encode the same causal relationships that equations describe. A shift or slope must be translated into a variable change.
    Why learners miss it Easiest clueStudents can verbalize the concept but become disoriented when axes, loops or curves replace prose. Name the axes and controlled variable first. Then predict the direction before looking at options.
    3. Pressure, flow and resistance unify cardio, pulmonary and renal physiology Deep explanationAcross organ systems, flow depends on a driving pressure gradient and resistance. Geometry, tone, compliance and downstream pressure change how much flow occurs and where pressures accumulate.
    Why learners miss it Easiest clueThe equations are often memorized separately by organ system, hiding the shared physical model. Gradient drives; resistance limits. Use that sentence before system-specific details.
