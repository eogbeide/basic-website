    YEAR 2 | PAGE 26E - STROKE, SEIZURE, HEADACHE + LOCALIZATION

    LOCALIZE BEFORE DIAGNOSING DEFICIT -> NEUROANATOMIC LEVEL -> SIDE -> VASCULAR / ELECTRICAL / STRUCTURAL MECHANISM -> TEST -> THERAPY. Before naming a

    disease, decide cortex vs subcortex vs brainstem vs spinal cord vs peripheral nerve/NMJ/muscle; then use time course and focality to narrow the mechanism.

    1

    Stroke Syndromes: MCA, ACA, ICA, PCA + Vertebrobasilar - Ninja Nerd~1 hr 17
    Maps vascular territories to motor, sensory, language, visual, brainstem and cerebellar deficits; excellent for lesion localization before memorizing stroke labels.

    2

    Acute Ischemic Stroke - Ninja Nerd~1 hr 15
    Ischemic core/penumbra, thrombotic vs embolic mechanisms, imaging sequence, reperfusion logic, complications and secondary prevention.

    3

    Seizures + Status Epilepticus - Ninja Nerd~1 hr 41
    Focal vs generalized seizure classification, clinical semiology, EEG/diagnostic reasoning, treatment and status epilepticus priorities.

    4

    Migraine Medications + Mechanism - Ninja Nerd~41 min
    Trigemino-vascular pathophysiology, acute vs preventive therapy, triptans/CGRP agents and contraindication logic for headache questions.

    CLOSED-BOOK NEURO CHECK

    • Given a focal deficit, name the anatomic level and side before the diagnosis; then predict the vascular territory or tract involved.
    • For seizure questions, classify focal vs generalized, awareness, motor/nonmotor pattern, provoking cause and status risk before choosing a drug.
    • For headache, screen for red flags first: sudden thunderclap, fever/meningismus, focal deficit, papilledema, pregnancy/hypercoagulability, cancer/immunosuppression or new late-onset pattern.
    • Distinguish UMN, LMN, NMJ and muscle patterns using tone, reflexes, sensory findings, fatigability and distribution.
