E. FAMILY MEDICINE REVIEW MAP — 7. Screening applies to asymptomatic people; symptoms switch the pathway to diagnosis DEEP EXPLANATION A screening recommendation answers whether to test an asymptomatic person at a defined risk level. Once symptoms, abnormal examination findings or a prior abnormal

    result appear, the question is diagnostic evaluation, which may use different tests, intervals and urgency.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Students memorize screening ages and apply them mechanically even when the stem has already crossed into symptomatic disease. EASIEST CLUE Ask screening or diagnosis? The moment a relevant symptom or abnormal finding appears, stop reciting the screening schedule. — 8. Safe outpatient care requires a follow-up and escalation plan DEEP EXPLANATION Primary care frequently chooses conservative treatment or watchful waiting because the patient is stable, risk is manageable and follow-up can detect failure. That choice is

    incomplete without a time horizon, expected trajectory and clear return/escalation thresholds.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Hospital-based training creates an "admit/image now" reflex; exam questions exploit the difference between stable ambulatory care and unstable disease. EASIEST CLUE Outpatient is safe when you can answer: when will I reassess, what should improve, and what makes me escalate? — 9. Chronic disease is a trajectory + context problem, not one abnormal number DEEP EXPLANATION Hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia, obesity, depression and other chronic problems are managed by trends, end-organ risk, adherence, access, adverse effects,

    comorbidities and patient goals. A single value may prompt action, but the longitudinal plan determines whether the action is appropriate. WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Students jump from a threshold to a medication without asking whether the measurement is valid, whether treatment is being taken, or what comorbidity changes the target/choice.

    EASIEST CLUE Use control -> complications -> context -> change -> monitoring before choosing the next medication. — 10. Prevention is active management and must be age/risk/current-guideline specific DEEP EXPLANATION Vaccination, tobacco/alcohol counseling, cancer screening, cardiovascular-risk reduction, osteoporosis prevention, reproductive planning and behavioral-health screening

    can prevent more morbidity than adding another diagnostic test. Recommendations evolve, so the exam and real care require current guideline awareness.

    EASIEST CLUE Use control -> complications -> context -> change -> monitoring before choosing the next medication. — WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Prevention is often postponed because acute complaints feel more clinically dramatic, and learners rely on outdated memorized schedules. EASIEST CLUE At every stable visit ask: what prevention is due now, and what risk factor changes the recommendation? F.
