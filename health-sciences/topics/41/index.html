    C. WHAT SCHOOL EXAMS COMMONLY REQUIRE

    ResourceDescriptionLink
    Tracts / spinal cordTrace pathway, modality, crossing and level; use pattern of motor/sensory loss to localize cord, root, plexus or peripheral nerve.
    Brainstem / cranial nervesCombine CN deficits, gaze/pupil findings and long-tract signs to identify the side and approximate brainstem level.
    Cortex / vascular territoriesLocalize aphasia, neglect, visual fields, motor/sensory deficits and higher cortical dysfunction; connect to arterial territories.
    Basal ganglia / cerebellumDifferentiate movement initiation/suppression disorders from coordination, gait and eye-movement abnormalities.
    Special senses / autonomicsLocalize visual, auditory/vestibular and pupillary abnormalities using pathway anatomy and relevant reflex arcs.
    Behavioral / psychiatryDistinguish normal variants from syndromes by duration, function and context; connect neurotransmitter/circuit logic to medications and adverse effects.

    D. 7-DAY SCHOOL-EXAM REVIEW CYCLE

    ResourceDescriptionLink
    Days 1-2Spinal cord + motor/sensory tracts. Draw every major pathway, crossing and lesion pattern from memory.
    Days 3-4Brainstem/CNs + cortex/vascular territories. Work mixed localization vignettes before looking at answer choices.
    Day 5Basal ganglia, cerebellum, special senses, autonomics, sleep and neurotransmitter systems; integrate exam findings with circuitry.
    Day 6Behavioral/developmental/psychiatric foundations + timed mixed school-style block. Include images and neuro-exam findings.
    Day 7Error-driven re-test using NEW cases. If localization still fails, redraw the pathway rather than rereading a disease list.

    D. 7-DAY SCHOOL-EXAM REVIEW CYCLE — E. NEUROSCIENCE ERROR-LOG TAXONOMY

    • Localization gap - recognized the disease name but could not identify the anatomic level from the findings.
    • Decussation/side gap - knew the tract but predicted the wrong side of the deficit.
    • Exam-sign gap - confused UMN/LMN, cerebellar, basal-ganglia, sensory or cranial-nerve patterns.
    • Neurotransmitter/circuit gap - memorized an association without understanding which pathway/function was altered.
    • Behavioral-pattern gap - ignored duration, functional impairment, developmental context, substance/medical causes or normal variation.
