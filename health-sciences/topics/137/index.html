    A. THE THREE-LANE YEAR 3 SYSTEM

    LaneDaily / weekly behaviorEvidence that it is working
    1. Clinical lanePre-round/prepare; see the patient; build a one-line problem representation; rank the differential; propose the next test and management step; follow results; present and close loops under supervision.Feedback improves; presentations are shorter and more prioritized; you anticipate deterioration and disposition needs.
    2. Shelf / COMAT laneMost clinical days: 10-20 rotation-specific questions or an equivalent small block; one larger timed block weekly; review every incorrect, guessed correct and low-confidence correct.Domain accuracy rises, repeat errors fall, timing stabilizes and management-sequence errors become less frequent.
    3. Cumulative laneKeep prior-rotation high-yield rules alive with brief spaced retrieval and periodic mixed questions; do not let one shelf replace everything learned before it.Old clerkship material remains retrievable when mixed questions reappear and Step 2 / Level 2 preparation becomes integration rather than relearning.

    C. PATIENT-TO-QUESTION TRANSFER TEMPLATE

    • Who / context? Age, setting, risk factors, medications, pregnancy status, immune status, recent procedure/exposure.
    • What syndrome + tempo? Acute vs subacute vs chronic; dominant organ system; stable vs unstable.
    • Dangerous alternative? The diagnosis that cannot safely be missed even if it is not most likely.
    • Best next step now? Stabilize, test, treat, consult, observe or discharge - not the eventual step later.
    • What changes disposition? Vital-sign trend, oxygen need, inability to tolerate PO, social support, follow-up reliability, procedure/monitoring need.
    • What will the shelf turn this into? Diagnostic sequence, management sequence, adverse effect, complication, prevention or prognosis.
