    C. HIGH-VALUE SYSTEM / SYNDROME MAP — 4. AKI is a mechanism + trajectory problem, not just a creatinine number DEEP EXPLANATION Internal-medicine questions ask why kidney function changed and what must be reversed now. Volume status, hemodynamics, nephrotoxins, obstruction, urinalysis/sediment and the time course separate

    prerenal/hemodynamic, intrinsic and postrenal patterns. Severity is defined by consequences as well as the creatinine.

    C. HIGH-VALUE SYSTEM / SYNDROME MAP — WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Students see a high creatinine and jump directly to a named renal disease or an invasive test. EASIEST CLUE Start with volume / perfusion -> medications/toxins -> obstruction -> urine pattern; then ask whether K, acid-base, volume or uremia creates an urgent indication for escalation. 5. GI bleed and cirrhosis questions separate resuscitation from source diagnosis DEEP EXPLANATION A patient with bleeding or decompensated liver disease may need stabilization and complication-specific treatment before definitive source localization. Cirrhosis questions also test recognition of ascites infection,

    encephalopathy, variceal disease, renal dysfunction and recurrent-prevention logic.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT The memorable procedure - endoscopy, paracentesis or imaging - distracts from the immediate physiology and treatment sequence. EASIEST CLUE Ask is the immediate problem blood loss, infection, pressure/portal complication, mental status or renal perfusion? Fix the dangerous physiology while pursuing the source. 6. Endocrine emergencies are ordered algorithms built from physiology DEEP EXPLANATION DKA/HHS, adrenal crisis and severe thyroid disease are not solved by naming the disorder alone. Fluids, electrolytes, hormone replacement or insulin may have a required order because one intervention changes the

    safety of the next. The same principle applies to transition and monitoring.
