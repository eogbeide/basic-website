    C. CLINICAL-SCIENCE WEIGHTING FROM THE SOURCE BLUEPRINT — 4. Integrated classification means the clinical-science percentages intentionally overlap DEEP EXPLANATION USMLE classifies many questions into more than one clinical science, so Medicine, Pediatrics, OB/GYN, Psychiatry and Surgery ranges can add to more than 100%. A

    postoperative PE, for example, can test Medicine, Surgery, diagnostic studies and management simultaneously.

    C. CLINICAL-SCIENCE WEIGHTING FROM THE SOURCE BLUEPRINT — WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Students try to convert overlapping percentages into a rigid 100-hour schedule and accidentally double-count or neglect cross-disciplinary decisions. EASIEST CLUE If the percentages add above 100%, think integration, not arithmetic error. 5. Medicine is the largest clinical-science contribution, but mixed readiness requires protected coverage of every clerkship DEEP EXPLANATION The source blueprint correctly emphasizes Medicine at 55-65%, but Pediatrics, OB/GYN, Psychiatry and Surgery remain substantial and often supply the age, pregnancy,

    procedure, safety or communication context that changes the answer.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT A high Medicine weight tempts learners to let lower-volume rotations decay, especially after those shelves are complete. EASIEST CLUE Keep a mixed core plus rotating weak-clerkship lane rather than studying Medicine alone. 6. Nutrition and social sciences are cross-cutting overlays, not isolated chapters DEEP EXPLANATION Current official system specifications explicitly give Nutrition 15-20% and Social Sciences/legal-ethical-professionalism/systems/patient safety 10-15%. These concepts can

    be co-coded with organ-system questions, so they should be practiced inside real clinical decisions rather than saved for a separate final review. WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Students interpret a content-outline row as a standalone subject and miss how nutrition, safety, ethics or systems reasoning modifies common cardiology, surgery, pediatrics or outpatient questions.

    EASIEST CLUE When reviewing any case, ask is there a nutrition, prevention, safety, ethics or systems layer hiding inside the organ-system problem?
