    D. 10 HIGH-DISCRIMINATION SIMULATION DECISIONS

    #Case typeBest simulation move
    1Shock / severe hypoxemiaStabilize physiology before broad testing; choose a monitored location and reassess immediately.
    2Chest pain with evolving instabilityUrgent stabilization + targeted cardiac evaluation; avoid waiting for complete outpatient-style workup.
    3Sepsis / serious infectionCultures when decision-relevant without delaying time-critical therapy; fluids/pressors/monitoring by physiology; source control.
    4DKA / major metabolic crisisSequence fluids, potassium assessment/repletion and insulin appropriately; monitor glucose/electrolytes/anion gap and disposition.
    5Acute neurologic deficitTime of onset, glucose/rapid exclusion of mimics, urgent imaging/eligibility decisions and monitored disposition.
    6Toxicologic presentationStabilize, identify toxidrome, use ECG/acid-base clues and give antidote/supportive treatment when indicated; avoid indiscriminate testing.
    7Postoperative deteriorationRecognize the dangerous syndrome first; test/treat in parallel when unstable; escalate level of care early.
    8Ambulatory chronic diseaseOptimize medication + monitoring + adherence + prevention; schedule follow-up and define escalation thresholds.
    9Pregnancy / reproductive emergencyMaternal stabilization, pregnancy/gestational context, fetal considerations and consultation/disposition in the correct sequence.
    10Safe dischargeClinical stability + medication reconciliation + pending-result risk + support + follow-up + return precautions.

    10. THE FIRST MANAGEMENT ERROR IS THE HIGHEST-VALUE REMEDIATION TARGET

    DEEP EXPLANATIONWHY STUDENTS MISS ITEASIEST CLUE
    A case may contain many imperfect choices, but the earliest error often causes the downstream cascade: delayed stabilization, wrong location, premature time advancement, low-value testing or unsafe disposition. Fix the causal error first.Reviewers can spend equal time on every missed detail and fail to repair the decision rule that actually changed the case trajectory.Ask: What was the first decision that made the rest of the case harder or less safe?

    E. ERROR -> REPAIR -> RETEST SYSTEM

    Error classRepairDelayed retest
    Delayed stabilizationWrite a 15-second instability checklist; repeat acute cases until ABC/time-critical actions are automatic.Different emergency, same stabilization decision.
    Shotgun testingFor every order, state the management consequence before clicking/selecting it.New case where fewer tests are safer.
    Wrong sequenceConvert the miss into an ordered 3-5 step algorithm.Same mechanism in a different presentation.
    No reassessmentAttach a monitor/recheck action to every major therapy.Case where treatment causes a new abnormality.
    Unsafe dispositionUse a closure checklist: stability, support, meds, pending risk, follow-up, precautions.Different diagnosis with the same discharge/admit decision.
    Format/mechanics errorPractice the official software/tutorial until response limits, case progression and time controls are automatic.Fresh official-style case without notes.

    E. ERROR -> REPAIR -> RETEST SYSTEM — FINAL MASTERY GATE

    Can I stabilize first, order only decision-relevant data, treat in the correct sequence, use time/location safely, reassess response, close the case appropriately, and execute the official CCS or CDM mechanics without hesitation? For CDM, can I stay within response limits and give concise, specific, safe answers?
