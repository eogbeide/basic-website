    THE CORE HABIT: LOCALIZE BEFORE YOU LABEL

    Neurology becomes manageable when every case starts with location. Before naming a disease, decide whether the problem is cortex, subcortex, brainstem, cerebellum, spinal cord, root/plexus, peripheral nerve, neuromuscular junction or muscle. Then use tempo and syndrome pattern to decide the likely mechanism and next test.

    A. NEUROLOGIC FIRST-PASS WORKFLOW

    StepWhat to doWhat it prevents
    1. Define the syndrome + tempoFocal deficit, altered cognition, seizure, headache, weakness, sensory loss, dizziness/ataxia or abnormal movement? Sudden, subacute, relapsing or progressive?Prevents mixing vascular, infectious, inflammatory, toxic/metabolic and degenerative processes.
    2. Mental status + languageLevel of consciousness, attention, orientation, memory, naming/repetition/comprehension, neglect and behavior.Separates diffuse encephalopathy from dominant/nondominant cortical dysfunction.
    3. Cranial nervesPupils/vision, eye movements, face, hearing, palate/voice, tongue and bulbar function.Finds brainstem/cavernous/orbital/cranial-nerve patterns and dangerous airway/swallow problems.
    4. Motor + reflexesBulk, tone, pronator drift, power pattern, DTRs, plantar response and fatigability.Distinguishes UMN vs LMN vs NMJ vs muscle.
    5. Sensory + coordination + gaitMap modality and distribution; test proprioception, cerebellar function, Romberg and gait.Identifies sensory level, peripheral pattern, dorsal-column/cerebellar disease and functional impact.
    6. Localize + choose one decisive testState anatomic level/side and the dangerous alternative; then select the test that can change urgent management.Prevents reflex CT/MRI ordering without a localization question.

    B. LOCALIZATION PATTERN TABLE

    LevelClues that point thereClassic discriminator
    CortexAphasia, neglect, gaze preference, cortical sensory loss, seizure, visual-field defect.Higher cortical sign + contralateral deficit.
