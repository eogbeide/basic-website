    F. GYNECOLOGIC PRESENTATIONS - BUILD THE DIFFERENTIAL BY SYNDROME

    PresentationHigh-yield discriminatorsManagement logic to practice
    Acute pelvic / lower abdominal painPregnancy possibility, hemodynamics, sudden vs gradual onset, unilateral pain, fever/discharge, peritoneal signs, adnexal mass.Pregnancy test early when relevant. Exclude ectopic pregnancy and ovarian torsion before settling on PID, cyst, endometriosis, GI or urinary causes.
    First-trimester bleedingPregnancy location/viability, pain, cervical findings, hemodynamics, beta-hCG/ultrasound relationship and Rh context.Separate threatened/early pregnancy loss from ectopic and other causes; unstable bleeding changes the sequence immediately.
    Abnormal uterine bleedingPregnancy status, age, bleeding severity, anemia, ovulatory pattern, medications/coagulopathy and structural risk.Stabilize heavy bleeding first, then organize causes as structural vs nonstructural (PALM-COEIN) and choose imaging/endometrial evaluation based on age/risk.
    Vaginal discharge / vulvar symptomsAppearance, odor, pH/microscopy or NAAT context, STI risk, pain, fever, ulcers/lesions and pregnancy status.Differentiate vaginitis, cervicitis/PID, STI and noninfectious disease; treat partners/testing when indicated and use trauma-informed sexual history.
    Pelvic mass / pressureAge, pregnancy, acute torsion symptoms, fibroid/adenomyosis pattern, ovarian features, ascites, weight loss or postmenopausal bleeding.Decide whether this is benign-pattern disease, an acute surgical threat or a malignancy workup requiring imaging/biopsy/specialist referral.
    Breast complaintAge, pregnancy/lactation, focal mass, infection signs, skin/nipple changes and screening history.Distinguish mastitis/abscess and benign mass patterns from findings requiring diagnostic imaging and cancer evaluation.

    G. REPRODUCTIVE HEALTH + PREVENTION

    DomainReasoning anchor
    ContraceptionStart with the patient's goals, effectiveness preference, contraindications and noncontraceptive benefits. Match method to medical eligibility rather than memorizing a one-size-fits-all hierarchy.
    InfertilityConfirm duration/context, ovulation, uterine/tubal factors and semen evaluation. Avoid evaluating only one partner or ordering advanced tests before the basic pathway.
    Cervical / gynecologic cancer preventionUse current age/risk-based screening guidance. Distinguish screening, diagnostic evaluation of abnormal results and surveillance after treatment.
    Menopause / osteoporosisSymptoms, bleeding pattern, bone/cardiovascular risk and medication contraindications. Postmenopausal bleeding is not assumed benign.
    Sexual violence / intimate partner violencePrioritize immediate safety, consent, trauma-informed examination, forensic options where applicable, STI/pregnancy prevention and mandated/institutional processes.

    G. REPRODUCTIVE HEALTH + PREVENTION — H. THREE EXAM REFLEXES THAT PREVENT COMMON MISSES

    • Pregnancy possibility first: a reproductive-age patient with pelvic pain or bleeding often needs pregnancy status established before the rest of the algorithm makes sense.
    • Stability before specificity: hemorrhage, sepsis or torsion can make the correct next step urgent even before every diagnostic detail is known.
    • Screening is not diagnosis: abnormal screening results trigger a different pathway from symptomatic disease; know what requires colposcopy, imaging, biopsy or referral conceptually and verify current intervals.
