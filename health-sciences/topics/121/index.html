    DISPOSITION — WHY PSYCHIATRY IS A SEQUENCING CLERKSHIP

    The diagnosis is rarely a single symptom. Build each encounter from behavior and context: stabilize urgent medical problems, obtain the patient story and collateral, describe the mental status exam, exclude delirium/substance/medication/medical causes when indicated, assess safety and capacity, then choose a treatment setting and follow-up plan.

    A. THE PSYCHIATRIC FIRST-PASS FRAME

    StepAsk yourselfOutput
    1. Medical / behavioral stabilityIs there delirium, intoxication/withdrawal, overdose, severe agitation, catatonia, eating-disorder medical instability, or another condition requiring immediate medical care?Stabilization and supervised escalation before diagnostic refinement.
    2. Reason for encounter + collateralWhy is the patient here, who is concerned, what changed from baseline, and what do family/EMS/chart/medication records add?Problem representation with reliable timeline and context.
    3. Psychiatric history + MSESymptoms, duration, function, past episodes, treatment response, substances, trauma, sleep, medical/neurologic history; describe current MSE.Syndrome defined by observed findings + longitudinal history.
    4. Medical / substance rule-outCould this be delirium, endocrine/neurologic disease, medication effect, intoxication or withdrawal rather than a primary psychiatric disorder?Focused exam/testing only when it changes the differential or safety plan.
    5. Safety + capacitySuicidal/self-harm thoughts, violence risk, grave disability, access to lethal means, protective factors; can the patient understand/appreciate/reason/communicate a choice?Explicit risk formulation and decision-making assessment.
    6. Treatment + dispositionWhat level of care is safe: outpatient, urgent follow-up, voluntary/involuntary evaluation per local law, inpatient psychiatry, or medical admission?Defensible supervised plan + contingency + follow-up.

    B. MENTAL STATUS EXAM - DESCRIBE BEFORE YOU LABEL

    DomainHigh-yield observation / question
    Appearance / behaviorGrooming, eye contact, psychomotor activity, cooperation, abnormal movements, response to internal stimuli.
    SpeechRate, volume, fluency, latency, spontaneity, pressure.
    Mood / affectPatient-stated mood; observed range, intensity, stability, congruence and reactivity of affect.
    Thought process / contentLinear vs tangential/circumstantial/disorganized; delusions, obsessions, guilt, hopelessness, SI/HI and other safety content.
    PerceptionHallucinations/illusions; modality, context, insight and whether symptoms occur with substances, sleep transitions or delirium.
    CognitionAttention first; orientation, memory/language/executive testing as clinically indicated. Fluctuating inattention should trigger delirium reasoning.
    Insight / judgmentRecognition of illness/need for care; ability to anticipate consequences and make safe decisions.
