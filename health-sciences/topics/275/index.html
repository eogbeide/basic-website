    F. ASSESSMENT -> ACTION ALGORITHM — 7. Assessment results should trigger diagnosis and remediation - not a total curriculum reset DEEP EXPLANATION A checkpoint is most useful when it reveals whether prior interventions worked and which error patterns remain. Broadly changing resources or schedules after every form destroys

    the ability to learn which intervention caused improvement.

    F. ASSESSMENT -> ACTION ALGORITHM — WHY STUDENTS MISS IT A disappointing score creates urgency; students may respond by abandoning a working plan or adding multiple new resources at once. EASIEST CLUE Change the plan only where persistent evidence identifies a persistent bottleneck. 8. Assessment spacing should allow an intervention and delayed confirmation DEEP EXPLANATION If assessments are taken too close together, the learner has little time to repair the underlying causes and the forms become repeated measurement rather than instruction. The

    right spacing depends on school policy, test date and the amount of remediation required.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Taking another form can feel productive and can temporarily relieve uncertainty, even when nothing meaningful changed between measurements. EASIEST CLUE Before scheduling the next form ask: what will be different by then? 9. Endurance now means repeated 30-minute resets across a 9-hour day, not only surviving long blocks DEEP EXPLANATION Current Step 2 CK uses sixteen 30-minute blocks in a 9-hour session, with no more than 20 questions per block. Execution therefore includes repeated transitions: close a block,

    mentally reset, use breaks deliberately and restart attention without carrying rumination forward.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Students may still rehearse the older long-block feel or equate endurance only with total question count, missing the cognitive cost of frequent block boundaries. EASIEST CLUE Practice short-block pacing + clean resets + planned breaks, while preserving the full-day stamina requirement.
