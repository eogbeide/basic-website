    D. TOP 10 BLUEPRINT MISTAKES - #1 TO #3

    # / CONCEPTDEEP EXPLANATIONWHY STUDENTS MISS ITEASIEST CLUE
    1. Treating percentage ranges as exact question countsOfficial ranges are flexible test-construction specifications and can change. Use them to prevent blind spots, not to predict an exact number of questions on your form.A numerical blueprint feels precise enough to reverse-engineer the test.Use ranges for coverage assurance, not question-count forecasting.
    2. Using system weight alone to allocate study timeTime should reflect blueprint coverage and your recurring weakness, fragility, retention and transfer errors. A lower-range domain can deserve more repair if it is repeatedly missed.High-range systems feel automatically more valuable.Allocate by coverage x weakness x recurrence, not coverage alone.
    3. Training only text vignettesStep 1 explicitly requires interpretation of graphic/tabular material and gross/microscopic specimens. Mechanisms must survive changes in representation.Text Qbanks can create the illusion that content format does not matter.For each weak topic, ask: can I solve the graph, table, image and experiment version?
