    TRANSITION TO RESIDENCY + INTERN-LEVEL WORKFLOW

    ShareXinf

    YEAR 4 | PAGE 36A - SUPERVISED AUTONOMY -> CROSS-COVER -> ESCALATION -> CLOSED LOOPS

    The original transition page correctly framed the goal as closing the gap between supervised medical-student work and safe resident function. This expansion keeps that boundary explicit: practice intern-level thinking while decisions, orders and procedures remain under the supervision required by your role.

    THE NEW-INTERN OPERATING RULE

    Own the loop, not the patient alone: recognize the problem -> assess severity -> form a focused plan -> communicate/escalate -> execute permitted tasks -> reassess -> document what changed and what still needs follow-up.

    A. SHIFT FROM STUDENT HABITS TO INTERN-READY BEHAVIOR

    DomainStudent-level defaultIntern-ready supervised behavior
    Pre-roundingCollects data and reports it.Prioritizes overnight events, trend direction, active threats and what must happen before rounds.
    AssessmentProduces a broad differential.Names the leading diagnosis, dangerous alternative, uncertainty and the next decision that changes care.
    Tasks / ordersWaits for each instruction.Drafts safe, indication-linked orders for review; checks allergies, dose/route, organ function, interactions, monitoring and stop/hold criteria.
    ResultsNotes that a result returned.Interprets whether it changes diagnosis, treatment, disposition or urgency and closes the loop with the team.
    EscalationCalls when told.Recognizes instability early, states why the patient worries you and asks for a specific level of help.

    B. CROSS-COVER RESPONSE - 8 STEPS

    • 1. Triage: Is this routine, urgent or immediately life-threatening?
    • 2. Go bedside when needed: unstable vitals, new hypoxemia, chest pain, neurologic change, severe pain, bleeding or staff concern.
    • 3. Stabilize: ABCs, immediate monitoring and time-critical actions within your supervised role.
    • 4. Reconstruct context: diagnosis, procedure/day of illness, medications, fluids/I&O, recent labs/imaging and code status.
    • 5. Focus the differential: common + dangerous + iatrogenic/medication causes.
    • 6. Act: choose tests/treatments that change the next decision; avoid reflexive shotgun ordering.
    • 7. Reassess: response to intervention is new diagnostic data.
    • 8. Close the loop: update senior/attending/nursing team, give contingency plan, document and hand off unresolved items.
