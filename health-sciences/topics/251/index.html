    E. BROAD MEDICINE COVERAGE MAP — 7. Heme/onc questions often hinge on urgency before taxonomy DEEP EXPLANATION Anemia and coagulopathy require pattern recognition, but neutropenic fever, tumor lysis, hypercalcemia, cord compression and major treatment toxicities are management emergencies. The learner must recognize

    when the complication outranks a complete malignancy classification.

    E. BROAD MEDICINE COVERAGE MAP — WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Students trained on pathology categories keep naming diseases while the vignette is asking what must happen first. EASIEST CLUE If a cancer patient has fever, neurologic deficit, severe electrolyte change, bleeding or hemodynamic instability, first ask whether the complication itself is the tested problem. 8. Infection questions are source + host + timing + source control DEEP EXPLANATION The same organism behaves differently in an immunocompromised host, around devices or after hospitalization. Exam questions frequently test whether cultures/specimens, empiric coverage, source control and

    de-escalation occur in the right order without delaying urgent therapy in an unstable patient.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Memorizing organism-drug pairs ignores the clinical sequence and the host context. EASIEST CLUE Build the chain site/syndrome -> host/exposure -> dangerous pathogens -> best specimen/test -> empiric coverage -> source control -> narrow. 9. Rheumatology serology follows the phenotype DEEP EXPLANATION Autoantibodies and complement shift probability after the clinical pattern has been defined. Joint distribution, inflammatory features, skin/kidney/lung/nerve involvement and vessel size should come first; testing then

    confirms or distinguishes plausible branches.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT A positive antibody is memorable and can become an anchor even when the syndrome does not fit. EASIEST CLUE Before ordering or interpreting a rheumatologic test, say what phenotype am I trying to confirm or separate? 10. Disposition is part of the diagnosis and management question DEEP EXPLANATION Internal Medicine repeatedly asks whether a patient needs ICU-level monitoring, inpatient care, observation, specialty escalation or safe outpatient follow-up. Disposition depends on physiologic stability, trajectory,

    comorbidity, treatment needs and ability to complete follow-up - not on the disease label alone.
