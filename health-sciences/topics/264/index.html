    YEAR 3 | PAGE 64D - 10 HIGH-DISCRIMINATION CASES + CURRENT NBME / COMAT TRANSFER

    FINAL REVIEW SHOULD FORCE THE FIRST SAFE DECISION: PSYCH SAFETY / SECONDARY CAUSE -> NEURO LOCALIZATION / TEMPO -> PRIMARY-CARE SCREENING VS DIAGNOSIS -> NEXT STEP -> FOLLOW-UP / DISPOSITION. DO NOT LET A FAMILIAR LABEL BYPASS THE SEQUENCE. G. 10 HIGH-DISCRIMINATION SHELF / COMAT CASES

    #Case patternBest exam reasoning
    1Depressed patient reports active suicidal intent, a specific plan and access to means.Safety/disposition comes first: immediate supervised risk management and emergency psychiatric evaluation outrank medication fine-tuning.
    2Older hospitalized patient develops acute fluctuating inattention, disorganized thinking and sleep-wake reversal.Treat as delirium until proven otherwise: identify medical/medication causes and protect safety rather than diagnosing a primary psychotic disorder.
    3Patient has recurrent mood episodes with psychosis; stem asks schizophrenia vs mood disorder vs schizoaffective.Build the timeline: determine whether psychosis occurs only during mood episodes or also independently, then apply duration/function/exclusion criteria.
    4Sudden aphasia with contralateral face/arm weakness.Localize to dominant cortical/vascular territory first, then activate the acute stroke imaging/time pathway; do not start with chronic neurologic differentials.
    5Abrupt "worst headache of life" with vomiting and meningismus.Thunderclap is a red flag; prioritize emergent secondary-headache evaluation for subarachnoid hemorrhage rather than treating as routine migraine.
    6Ptosis/diplopia worsen through the day; sensation is intact and weakness is fatigable.Localize to the neuromuscular junction; assess bulbar/respiratory risk and use confirmatory testing/therapy appropriate to the syndrome.
    7Asymptomatic patient asks for a preventive test; no red flags or prior abnormal result.Use age/risk/current-guideline screening logic. Do not convert preventive care into a broad diagnostic workup without an indication.
    8Stable outpatient has persistently above-goal chronic-disease measurements but no emergency features.Validate trend/adherence/context, adjust the longitudinal plan and schedule monitoring; avoid an inpatient/emergency reflex when outpatient care is safe.
    9Adult has uncomplicated low-back pain, normal neurologic exam and no systemic/red-flag features.Use conservative outpatient management and follow-up rather than reflex imaging; escalate if red flags, neurologic deficits or failure-to-improve change the risk.
    10Stable adult presents for routine care with overdue vaccination/screening and modifiable cardiovascular risk.Prevention is the management task: update age/risk-appropriate services and counseling instead of ordering non-indicated broad testing.
