SOURCE-DERIVED 4-6 WEEK TEMPLATE - PRESERVED + OPERATIONALIZED

    PhaseSource-derived focusOperational output
    Opening 3-4 daysBaseline CCSSA or school-approved assessment; build list by clerkship/system + error type.One ranked weakness matrix, one primary Qbank, defined review method, daily management/prevention/safety lane, assessment dates and stop rules.
    Weeks 1-2Mixed UWorld/AMBOSS blocks; repair earliest/weakest clerkships; daily management algorithms; prevention/ethics/patient safety included.Repair high-frequency decay while preserving mixed recognition; every recurring miss ends in retrieval + delayed confirmation.
    Weeks 2-4Mostly mixed timed blocks; one targeted half-day per weak area; second CCSSA; deliberate abstract/quality/safety review.Shift from repair toward transfer. Use assessment results to diagnose persistent bottlenecks, not to restart the curriculum.
    Weeks 4-5High-frequency missed presentations; third assessment if needed; full-day endurance simulation; review notes from questions rather than rewatching broad content.Narrow the error list, stabilize timing and rehearse current shorter-block execution.
    Final 5-7 daysRecurring errors, vaccines/screening, biostats/ethics, emergency algorithms, common medication adverse effects, official interface; taper volume.Consolidate, protect sleep, remove execution surprises and stop broad resource expansion.

    B. OPENING 3-4 DAYS - BUILD THE BASELINE DASHBOARD — 1. A baseline assessment is a calibration signal - not a complete prescription DEEP EXPLANATION A CCSSA or school-approved baseline estimates current transfer under standardized conditions. The dedicated schedule should also use question-level error categories, clerkship

    decay, timing behavior and recurrence. Two students with the same total score may need very different interventions.

    B. OPENING 3-4 DAYS - BUILD THE BASELINE DASHBOARD — WHY STUDENTS MISS IT One score is psychologically powerful, so learners may let the lowest content bar or the overall number dictate every study hour without checking why the misses occurred. EASIEST CLUE Ask what produced the score? Then schedule the mechanism of failure, not merely the label on the report. 2. A dedicated plan needs operations and feedback loops - not only a calendar DEEP EXPLANATION A useful plan specifies what happens after a miss: classify the error, select the repair tool, retrieve the rule from memory, and confirm it later on a new mixed question. The calendar

    provides time; the operating loop produces learning.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Hour-by-hour schedules feel organized even when they only list resources. They often fail to define what counts as repaired or when to stop reviewing. EASIEST CLUE Every study block should have an input, decision rule, output and retest. 3. Recurring and decayed high-frequency material outranks rare new detail early in dedicated DEEP EXPLANATION The opening phase has the highest leverage when it restores common presentations and management pathways that repeatedly fail across clerkships or mixed questions. These

    gaps affect many future vignettes and often interact with timing and confidence.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Students may chase novel or memorable zebras because they feel more concrete than repairing familiar topics they thought were already learned. EASIEST CLUE Prioritize frequent + recurring + management-changing before rare + isolated.
