    B. TOP 10 MOST-MISSED SYSTEM CONCEPTS - #1 TO #3

    # / CONCEPTDEEP EXPLANATIONWHY STUDENTS MISS ITEASIEST CLUE
    1. Localize GI disease before naming itGI reasoning becomes easier when the first question is where the failure lives: esophagus/stomach, small bowel, colon, pancreas, biliary tree or liver. The same symptom can come from different compartments, but anatomy constrains the differential and test choice.Students jump from a symptom such as diarrhea or abdominal pain directly to a disease list and miss the anatomic discriminator.Ask which compartment must fail to produce this pattern?
    2. Diarrhea / malabsorption is a mechanism problemOsmotic, secretory, inflammatory and malabsorptive processes differ in fasting response, stool content, mucosal injury and nutrient consequences. Fat malabsorption specifically points toward bile, pancreatic lipase, mucosa or lymphatic transport.Learners memorize named causes without linking them to what remains in the lumen or what the mucosa cannot absorb.Think water follows what stayed in the lumen, and deficiencies reveal what was not absorbed.
    3. Liver tests are pattern + function, not a single “LFT”AST/ALT reflect hepatocyte injury; alkaline phosphatase/cholestatic markers point toward bile-flow problems; bilirubin localization matters; albumin and PT/INR reflect synthetic function over different time scales.Students treat all liver abnormalities as interchangeable and miss the distinction between injury pattern and liver function.First ask injury, obstruction, bilirubin handling, or synthetic failure?
