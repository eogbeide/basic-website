    PLAN — THE OUTPATIENT EXAM TRAP

    The wrong answer is often not a wrong diagnosis - it is the right test or treatment used too early. In ambulatory care, first decide whether the patient is sick enough to need ED/hospital escalation, then use focused history and exam to separate self-limited disease from conditions that justify testing, antibiotics, imaging, procedures or specialty referral.

    C. COMMON AMBULATORY PRESENTATIONS - FIRST DECISION

    PresentationRed flags / discriminatorsFamily-medicine next-step logic
    Cough / URI / sore throatHypoxemia, severe dyspnea, focal lung findings, dehydration, immunocompromise; viral pattern vs pneumonia vs asthma/COPD vs strep.Avoid automatic antibiotics or imaging. Test/treat when the pretest probability or severity justifies it; escalate respiratory distress.
    Dysuria / urinary symptomsPregnancy, fever/flank pain, obstruction, male anatomy, STI symptoms, recurrent/complicated history.Separate uncomplicated cystitis from pyelonephritis, STI/prostatitis or obstruction; choose urine testing/culture based on complexity.
    Headache / dizzinessThunderclap, focal deficit, fever/meningismus, trauma, pregnancy/postpartum, new older-age pattern, papilledema.Do not image every headache. Red flags change the sequence; otherwise classify the syndrome and treat appropriately.
    Abdominal / GI symptomsPeritoneal signs, GI bleed, dehydration, weight loss, persistent vomiting, pregnancy possibility, age-specific risk.Rule out time-sensitive disease before labeling functional or self-limited illness; use targeted labs/imaging only when they change management.
    Low back / joint painNeurologic deficit, bowel/bladder dysfunction, fever, cancer/infection risk, trauma, inflammatory clues, hot swollen joint.Mechanical pain usually needs conservative care first; red flags, suspected fracture, infection or neurologic compromise justify urgent imaging/workup.
    Rash / skin lesionMucosal involvement, systemic toxicity, rapidly progressive pain, immunocompromise, concerning pigmented lesion, cellulitis vs abscess.Describe morphology + distribution before naming the disease; drain when appropriate, biopsy/refer suspicious lesions and escalate severe reactions.
    Ear / eye / sinus complaintsVision loss, severe eye pain, orbital findings, mastoid signs, neurologic deficit, severe systemic illness.Differentiate self-limited/inflammatory disease from bacterial infection and vision-threatening pathology; avoid reflex antibiotics.
    Minor injury / woundNeurovascular compromise, tendon injury, foreign body, open fracture, bite, contamination, tetanus status.Assess function and neurovascular status first; decide imaging, irrigation/closure, prophylaxis and follow-up.

    D. DERMATOLOGY + MSK - PATTERN BEFORE LABEL

    ProblemWhat separates the optionsHigh-yield action
    Inflammatory vs infectious skin diseasePruritus vs pain, warmth, fluctuance, scale, vesicles, distribution, systemic symptoms.Do not call every red lesion cellulitis; abscess usually requires drainage, while inflammatory disease needs the correct anti-inflammatory strategy.
    Pigmented / changing lesionABCDE-type change, bleeding/ulceration, ugly-duckling pattern, risk factors.Suspicious lesions need biopsy/referral rather than reassurance from one benign feature.
    Hot swollen jointSeptic arthritis vs crystal disease vs inflammatory flare.When infection is plausible, synovial fluid analysis precedes steroid injection; do not be reassured by a history of gout alone.
    Shoulder / knee painTrauma, ROM pattern, instability, strength, effusion, mechanical symptoms, referred pain.Use anatomy + examination to localize before ordering MRI; many presentations start with conservative treatment.
    Back painMechanical pattern vs radiculopathy vs cauda equina, fracture, infection, malignancy.Imaging is not routine without red flags or persistent/progressive concerning features.
