PSYCHIATRY FIRST-PASS REVIEW MAP — 1. Safety and secondary causes come before the psychiatric label DEEP EXPLANATION A patient with agitation, hallucinations, depression or bizarre behavior may have primary psychiatric illness, delirium, intoxication/withdrawal, medication toxicity, neurologic

    disease or another medical cause. The first task is therefore safety, attention/level of consciousness, vital instability and a focused search for secondary causes before assigning a chronic psychiatric diagnosis. WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Familiar symptom words such as "hallucinations" or "depressed" trigger pattern recognition too early, and learners can ignore fluctuating attention, autonomic signs or immediate self-harm risk. EASIEST CLUE Ask dangerous or secondary first? If attention fluctuates, vitals are abnormal, onset is abrupt or there is active self-harm/violence risk, pause the diagnostic timeline and address safety/medical causes.

PSYCHIATRY FIRST-PASS REVIEW MAP — 2. Psychiatric look-alikes are separated by timeline + function + mood-psychosis relationship DEEP EXPLANATION Many disorders share sleep change, low mood, agitation, psychosis or anxiety. Diagnostic discrimination comes from duration, degree of functional impairment, whether

    psychosis occurs only during mood episodes or also outside them, and whether substances/medical disease better explain the syndrome.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Students memorize criteria in isolated lists but do not build a timeline, so vignettes with overlapping symptoms feel interchangeable. EASIEST CLUE Draw a one-line timeline: onset -> duration -> mood episodes -> psychosis inside/outside mood -> function -> exclusions. — 3. Psychopharmacology questions often hinge on toxicity, interaction or monitoring DEEP EXPLANATION Knowing the diagnosis is only half the question. Antidepressants, antipsychotics, lithium/mood stabilizers, anxiolytics and sedative-hypnotics can produce distinctive toxic

    syndromes, metabolic effects, movement disorders, organ toxicity or dangerous withdrawal. Management choices therefore depend on mechanism plus patient-specific risk and monitoring.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Students memorize criteria in isolated lists but do not build a timeline, so vignettes with overlapping symptoms feel interchangeable. EASIEST CLUE Draw a one-line timeline: onset -> duration -> mood episodes -> psychosis inside/outside mood -> function -> exclusions. — WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Learners pair one drug with one diagnosis but underlearn the adverse effect that makes an otherwise reasonable option wrong. EASIEST CLUE Before choosing a psych drug, ask: what can this cause, what must I monitor, and what dangerous syndrome can it mimic? B.
