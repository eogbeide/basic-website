    C. BUILD ILLNESS SCRIPTS AROUND THE PRESENTING SYNDROME

    SyndromeHigh-yield discriminatorsManagement logic to practice
    Respiratory distress / coughAge, upper vs lower airway sound, wheeze/stridor/crackles, work of breathing, oxygenation, feeding, viral context, focal exam.Decide supportive care vs bronchodilator/steroid/antibiotic pathway vs escalation; recognize impending respiratory failure.
    Fever / infectionAge, appearance, source, immunization status, duration, focal findings, meningismus, perfusion and host risk.The younger or more toxic the child, the lower the threshold for serious bacterial infection evaluation and supervised escalation.
    Vomiting / abdominal painBilious vs nonbilious, blood, stooling, episodic vs persistent pain, distention, hydration, age-specific surgical diagnoses.Separate self-limited illness from obstruction, intussusception, appendicitis, torsion, DKA, intracranial or metabolic causes.
    Dehydration / diarrheaIntake/output, tears/mucosa, capillary refill, mental status, weight change, urine output and electrolyte risk.Estimate severity clinically; use oral/enteral rehydration when appropriate and IV resuscitation when perfusion or tolerance requires it.
    Seizure / altered mental statusAge, fever, duration/focality, recovery, trauma/toxin exposure, glucose and neurologic exam.Stabilize first; distinguish simple febrile seizure patterns from CNS infection, epilepsy, metabolic/toxic and structural disease.
    Murmur / cyanosis / poor feedingTiming, oxygenation, pulses, perfusion, growth, respiratory symptoms and whether physiology is ductal-dependent or shunt/obstruction related.Connect congenital physiology to the child's age and instability instead of memorizing lesion names alone.
    Growth / endocrine / chronic diseaseGrowth velocity, pubertal stage, BMI, family pattern, systemic symptoms, medications and psychosocial context.Differentiate normal variants from endocrine, GI, renal, genetic, inflammatory or nutritional disease; manage longitudinally.

    D. ADULT REFLEXES THAT FAIL IN PEDIATRICS

    • Normal is age-dependent: heart rate, respiratory rate, blood pressure, communication and examination technique change across childhood.
    • Weight is a clinical variable: medication and fluid decisions often require kg-based calculation; recheck units and maximum doses.
    • Appearance matters: feeding, interaction, consolability, work of breathing and perfusion can be more informative than one laboratory value.
    • Congenital disease can present late: poor feeding, failure to thrive, cyanosis, recurrent infection or exertional symptoms may be the clue.
    • Family history is not enough: pediatrics also requires school, nutrition, environment, safety, development and caregiver observations.
