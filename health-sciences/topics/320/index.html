    E. 10 HIGH-DISCRIMINATION DIAGNOSTIC-REASONING CASES

    #PatternBest reasoning move
    1Chest-pain label fits initially, but a later vascular / neurologic clue is discordant.Reopen the model; do not force the new clue into the anchor. Re-rank dangerous alternatives and use an appropriate supervised pathway.
    2 3A patient is handed off with “simple infection,” but fever pattern and new systemic findings do not fit. One abnormal imaging finding is identified, yet major symptoms remain unexplained.Strip away the inherited label; rebuild the representation from raw data and trajectory. Avoid search satisficing; ask whether the finding explains the entire syndrome or is incidental / incomplete.
    4 5A recent memorable rare diagnosis resembles the current case superficially. A patient demographic or behavioral history frames the encounter before the history/exam is complete.Reset to base rate + discriminators; compare the rare diagnosis against the strongest common alternatives. Neutralize the frame; use the same syndrome-based reasoning and danger screen you would use without that label.
    6 7A test result is abnormal, but the clinical phenotype and trajectory strongly conflict with it. The leading diagnosis is common, but the patient has one high-consequence feature that does not belong.Check test limitations, timing and alternative explanations; do not let one result replace the model. Keep “most likely” and “must not miss” as separate lanes until the dangerous alternative is safely addressed.
    8 9A diagnosis has appeared in several prior notes, but the original evidence was weak. A disease presentation is atypical for the learner’s mental prototype.Prevent diagnostic momentum: independently reconstruct the case and verify the label against current evidence. Avoid representativeness error; use criteria, mechanism and probability rather than textbook appearance.
    10The patient fails to improve as the current diagnosis predicts.Treat non-response / trajectory as new diagnostic evidence; perform a timeout and update the differential.

    F. FINAL DIAGNOSTIC-REASONING MASTERY GATE

    GatePass condition
    Problem representation Differential structureCan compress the case while preserving tempo, severity, context and decisive findings. Keeps most likely, dangerous alternative and plausible competitor visible until safely resolved.
    Probability Bias interruptionUses base rate as a starting point and updates with discriminating evidence. Can recognize anchoring/closure/framing/search failure and apply a concrete forcing function.
    Testing CalibrationOrders/interprets tests according to decision value, pretest probability and clinical fit. Confidence is proportional to evidence; uncertainty is explicit rather than hidden.
    Reassessment CommunicationTrajectory, response, new results and follow-up can reopen the diagnosis. Can tell patient/team what is known, unknown, dangerous and what will trigger reconsideration.
    FeedbackHas a way to learn the eventual outcome and compare it with the original reasoning.
