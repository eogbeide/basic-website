A. PERIOPERATIVE / POSTOPERATIVE REVIEW MAP — 1. Pre-op evaluation is a management question, not a ritual clearance checklist DEEP EXPLANATION The useful preoperative test or consultation is the one that can change operative timing, perioperative monitoring, anesthesia strategy or risk reduction. Emergency source

    control can outrank exhaustive optimization when delay is more dangerous than the comorbidity.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Learners often equate "more risk" with "more testing" and order broad studies that do not alter the decision. EASIEST CLUE Ask: Will this result change whether, when, or how the patient goes to the OR? If not, it is unlikely to be the next-step answer. — 2. Postoperative deterioration should be solved by syndrome and physiology - not by a postoperative-day mnemonic DEEP EXPLANATION Fever, tachycardia, hypoxemia, hypotension, oliguria or delirium require a fresh differential based on severity, procedure, devices, medications, source and trajectory. The

    same symptom can reflect hemorrhage, infection, PE, aspiration, medication effect or another emergency.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT The familiar "post-op fever by day" shortcut can override localizing clues and physiologic instability. EASIEST CLUE Start with what is failing now? Perfusion, oxygenation, ventilation, source control, renal output or cognition - then localize the cause. — 3. Fluids, electrolytes and nutrition are different jobs that require reassessment DEEP EXPLANATION Resuscitation restores perfusion, maintenance replaces ordinary needs, and replacement matches abnormal ongoing losses. Nutrition asks whether the gut works, what

    route is safe and whether refeeding/metabolic complications require monitoring. Every intervention should be followed by objective reassessment.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT The familiar "post-op fever by day" shortcut can override localizing clues and physiologic instability. EASIEST CLUE Start with what is failing now? Perfusion, oxygenation, ventilation, source control, renal output or cognition - then localize the cause. — WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Students may treat "low urine" or "post-op" as automatic indications for more IV fluid and may confuse volume support with nutrition. EASIEST CLUE Name the job first: resuscitation, maintenance, replacement, or feeding. Then ask what response would prove the plan is working. B.
