    YEAR 3 | PAGE 62D - 10 HIGH-DISCRIMINATION CASES + NBME / COMAT TRANSFER

    FINAL REVIEW SHOULD FORCE SEQUENCING: RECOGNIZE INSTABILITY -> DEFINE THE SYNDROME / CHIEF COMPLAINT -> RANK DANGEROUS ALTERNATIVES -> CHOOSE THE NEXT TEST OR PROCEDURE -> TREAT -> REASSESS -> SOURCE CONTROL / DISPOSITION. G. 10 HIGH-DISCRIMINATION SHELF / COMAT CASES

    #Case patternBest exam reasoning
    1Post-op day 1 tachycardia + falling BP + increasing drain blood.Treat as hemorrhagic instability: resuscitate and escalate to hemorrhage/source control; do not begin with a low-yield broad fever workup.
    2Post-op oliguria after major surgery; hypotension and rising creatinine.Assess perfusion/volume, catheter patency and trajectory; fluid is a hypothesis to test, not an automatic reflex.
    3Diffuse abdominal pain + rigidity + free air.Perforation/peritonitis: resuscitation + antibiotics + urgent source control outrank further diagnostic refinement.
    4Blunt trauma, hypotension, positive FAST.Instability + localizable intra-abdominal bleeding: move toward definitive hemorrhage control rather than routine CT.
    5Small-bowel obstruction with fever, tachycardia, focal tenderness and metabolic concern.Recognize strangulation/ischemia risk; uncomplicated-obstruction algorithms no longer apply.
    6Chest pain + diaphoresis + ischemic ECG changes.Stabilize and activate the urgent ACS/reperfusion pathway; do not delay with lower-yield confirmatory testing.
    7Dyspnea + unilateral absent breath sounds + shock after trauma.Treat a tension physiology clinically when present; confirmatory imaging must not delay decompression.
    8AMS + bradypnea after opioid exposure.Ventilation/airway is the immediate threat; reverse clinically important opioid effect and reassess for recurrence.
    9Overdose + hypotension + wide QRS.Think sodium-channel blockade: ECG drives urgency and targeted antidotal/supportive treatment.
    10Pain improved after ED treatment but serial evaluation still shows unresolved high-risk disease.Disposition follows residual risk and trajectory, not symptom improvement alone; observation/admission may be the correct next step.
