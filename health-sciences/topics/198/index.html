    E. TOP 10 MOST-MISSED SYSTEM CONCEPTS - #4 TO #7

    # / CONCEPTDEEP EXPLANATIONWHY STUDENTS MISS ITEASIEST CLUE
    4. Primary vs central endocrine diseaseA target-gland failure produces low target hormone with an appropriately elevated trophic signal; central failure produces an inappropriately low/normal trophic signal. Hormone excess reverses the logic.Students memorize disease-specific lab pairs instead of deriving them from negative feedback.Draw three boxes with feedback arrows and ask where the defect sits.
    5. Dynamic testing asks whether a control system canSuppression tests ask whether an excessive axis can be turned down; stimulation tests ask whether a deficient axis can beThe names of tests are memorized without the physiologic question, so results feel arbitrary.Ask should this hormone suppress, stimulate, or stay
    respondactivated. The interpretation depends on what level of the axis should respond.autonomous?
    6. DKA vs HHS is physiology, not two listsDKA reflects marked insulin deficiency with lipolysis/ketogenesis and high-anion-gap acidosis; HHS has enough insulin to restrain major ketogenesis but not hyperglycemia/osmotic diuresis. Total-body K can be depleted even when serum K is initially normal/high.Students equate serum potassium with total-body stores and memorize cutoffs without understanding insulin/osmotic shifts.Separate ketones/acidosis from hyperosmolality/dehydration; then ask where K is, not only what the serum shows.
    7. Calcium disorders are a four-variable patternPTH, calcium, phosphate and vitamin D/renal function form a linked control system. The key is whether PTH is appropriate for the calcium concentration and what it does to kidney, bone and vitamin D activation.Single lab values are overlearned, while the physiologic relationship is underlearned.Start with calcium -> is PTH appropriately up or down?
