C. HIGH-YIELD PRESENTATION MAP — 4. Children can be critically ill before hypotension appears DEEP EXPLANATION Compensatory tachycardia, altered interaction, delayed capillary refill, poor urine output, cool extremities, worsening work of breathing or fatigue can precede late

    blood-pressure collapse. Appearance and trajectory are therefore central to pediatric acuity.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Adult training makes hypotension feel like the defining threshold for shock or respiratory failure. EASIEST CLUE Watch work of breathing + perfusion + mental status. Deteriorating effort with increasing fatigue is especially ominous. — 5. Weight-based care starts with the clinical job, not the arithmetic DEEP EXPLANATION Many fluids and drugs are kg-based, but the correct calculation depends on whether the child needs resuscitation, maintenance, replacement or a therapeutic dose. Route,

    maximum dose, contraindications and response still matter.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Students may correctly calculate a number for the wrong indication or forget to reassess after intervention. EASIEST CLUE First label the task - resuscitate, maintain, replace, or dose - then use kilograms and recheck units. — 6. Pediatric symptoms require age-specific danger thresholds and surgical awareness DEEP EXPLANATION Fever, seizure, vomiting, abdominal pain and poor feeding have different differentials and escalation thresholds across newborns, infants and older children. Bilious emesis,

    peritoneal signs, toxic appearance, focal neurologic findings or persistent shock can move the case out of a benign pathway immediately.
