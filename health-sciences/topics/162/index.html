TOP 10 MOST-MISSED RESOURCE-GOVERNANCE CONCEPTS - 6 TO 10

    ConceptWhat it really meansWhy students miss itEasiest clue
    6. Direct link != source qualityA correct playable link is necessary for usability but says nothing about whether the content is deep, accurate or well matched.Broken links are obvious; subtle instructional weakness is harder to detect.First validate fit; then validate link.
    7. One strong stack beats duplicationA primary concept source + primary Qbank + official assessment + targeted repair usually outperforms five overlapping lecture libraries.Resource abundance feels like optionality and safety.Every resource must have a non-duplicative job.
    8. Cross-source triangulationImportant curriculum decisions should be supported by more than one type of evidence: school design + national blueprint + teaching/practice needs.One prestigious source can become an anchor even when it covers only one dimension.Sequence, scope, teaching, assessment = different evidence.
    9. Exam alignment != curriculum completenessLicensing blueprints sample competency for licensure; they are not complete medical-school curricula.Board weighting is numeric and easy to organize around.Boards are a floor/assessment map, not the entire profession.
    10. Maintenance is curriculum workLinks break, formats change, videos disappear, schools redesign and guidelines update. Governance requires version control and recurring QA.Maintenance feels administrative rather than educational.If the learner cannot reach or trust it, it is not curriculum.
