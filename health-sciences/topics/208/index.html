    L. 10 HIGH-DISCRIMINATION CROSS-DOMAIN CASES

    #CASEANSWER LOGIC
    1Acute hot swollen knee + fever.Do not anchor on gout: classify as acute monoarthritis and exclude infection with synovial analysis when clinically appropriate.
    2Symmetric small-joint morning stiffness + anti-CCP.Inflammatory phenotype comes first; anti-CCP strengthens RA probability rather than creating the diagnosis by itself.
    3Young adult with malar/photosensitive pattern, cytopenias and renal findings.Multisystem immune-complex phenotype + serology/complement context supports SLE reasoning; organ involvement drives urgency.
    4New painful rash with mucosal erosions after a medication.Danger-first dermatology: mucosal/systemic involvement after a drug raises severe cutaneous-adverse-reaction concern before exact image labeling.
    5Pigmented lesion changing in asymmetry/border/color.Describe morphology/evolution, recognize malignancy concern, and use biopsy/pathology rather than visual certainty alone.
    6Aphasia + contralateral face/arm weakness.Localize to dominant cerebral hemisphere/MCA-type territory before naming stroke; predict associated cortical signs.
    7 8Ipsilateral cranial-nerve finding + contralateral body weakness. Fluctuating weakness improves with use? vs worsens with use?Crossed findings strongly suggest a brainstem localization; crossing logic is the discriminator. Use reflexes, sensation, autonomics and fatigability to separate NMJ patterns such as MG vs Lambert-Eaton from neuropathy/myopathy.
    9Psychosis with fluctuating attention and acute medical illness.Delirium/medical cause must be considered before primary psychotic disorder; attention and fluctuation are high-value clues.
    10Antipsychotic exposure + rigidity, fever and autonomic instability.Medication emergency pattern: mechanism/time course favor NMS-type toxicity over simple psychiatric relapse.
