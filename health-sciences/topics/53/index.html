    C. WHAT OPP / OMM SCHOOL ASSESSMENTS COMMONLY REQUIRE — Structural exam

    Identify landmarks; assess TART/tissue texture, symmetry and ROM; state the finding clearly and document the somatic dysfunction.

    C. WHAT OPP / OMM SCHOOL ASSESSMENTS COMMONLY REQUIRE — Spine / ribs

    Diagnose OA/AA/cervical, thoracic/lumbar Type I vs II and rib dysfunctions; connect key motion findings to the named diagnosis.

    Pelvis / sacrum

    Interpret ASIS/PSIS, standing/seated flexion, spring/sphinx and sacral-axis findings according to the school-taught diagnostic model.

    Technique setup

    Select an appropriate family, position patient/operator correctly, engage barrier/ease, give clear instructions, monitor and reassess.

    Safety / consent

    Screen contraindications, explain the maneuver, obtain consent, maintain draping/comfort and stop when symptoms or findings make treatment unsafe.

    Viscerosomatic / lymphatic

    Connect autonomic levels, Chapman/viscerosomatic associations and lymphatic concepts to a clinical scenario without replacing standard medical evaluation.

    D. 7-DAY OMM LAB / PRACTICAL REVIEW CYCLE — Days 1-2

    Rebuild regional diagnosis maps: cervical/thoracic/lumbar/ribs. Practice naming diagnosis -> ease -> barrier before touching a partner.

    D. 7-DAY OMM LAB / PRACTICAL REVIEW CYCLE — Days 3-4

    Pelvis + sacrum + extremities. Run complete diagnostic sequences with a partner and compare findings with faculty/official lab expectations.

    Day 5

    Technique families: ME, counterstrain, MFR/soft tissue, articulatory/FPR/BLT and school-taught HVLA. Emphasize setup + contraindications + reassessment.

    Day 6

    Timed mock practical: random region -> diagnosis -> treatment choice -> setup -> verbal safety check -> reassessment/documentation.

    Day 7

    Targeted repair only. Repeat missed diagnoses or setups on NEW partners/scenarios with instructor feedback; do not reinforce an incorrect motor pattern.

    E. OMM ERROR-LOG TAXONOMY

    • Landmark/palpation gap - hand placement or tissue interpretation is inconsistent, so the downstream diagnosis is unreliable.
    • Biomechanics gap - diagnosis label known but ease/barrier, Fryette/rib/pelvis/sacrum logic is reversed.
    • Setup gap - correct diagnosis and technique family, but patient/operator position or activating force is wrong.
    • Safety gap - contraindication, consent, neurologic/vascular red flag or reassessment was missed.
    • Integration gap - OMM fact recalled in isolation but not connected to the patient presentation, anatomy or conventional workup.

    SCHOOL-LAB / PRACTICAL MASTERY GATE

    • Proceed when you can diagnose unfamiliar partners consistently, explain why the diagnosis fits, choose a safe technique, set it up correctly and demonstrate improvement/reassessment under faculty supervision.
