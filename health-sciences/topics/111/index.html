    E. TRAUMA FIRST-PASS ALGORITHM

    StepCore questionExam / clinical logic
    1. Primary surveyIs there an immediate airway, breathing, circulation, disability or exposure threat?Treat life threats during the survey; do not delay lifesaving intervention for a perfect diagnosis.
    2. Hemodynamic statusStable, transient responder or persistently unstable?Stability determines how much diagnostic imaging the patient can safely tolerate before definitive control.
    3. Identify bleeding / injury sourceChest? abdomen/pelvis? long bone? external? retroperitoneal? neurologic injury?Use bedside exam and focused testing to localize the dangerous branch of the differential.
    4. FAST / CT / OR / IRWhat test or destination will change management now?A stable patient can often undergo definitive imaging; an unstable patient with a clear operative source may need immediate control.
    5. Secondary survey + reassessmentWhat injuries were missed and is the patient responding as expected?Recheck after every intervention; deterioration is new diagnostic information.

    F. ACUTE ABDOMEN - LEARN THE SIX SURGICAL SYNDROMES

    SyndromeClues to prioritizeDecision direction
    ObstructionPain/distention, vomiting, obstipation; prior surgery, hernia, malignancy.Resuscitate/decompress when appropriate; distinguish uncomplicated obstruction from strangulation/ischemia or perforation.
    PerforationSudden severe pain, peritonitis, free air or contaminated viscus.Antibiotics/resuscitation plus urgent source control when perforation is confirmed or strongly suspected.
    IschemiaPain out of proportion, vascular risk, shock or rising metabolic concern.Time-sensitive vascular/surgical evaluation; do not be reassured by an early benign exam when risk is high.
    Inflammation / infectionAppendix, gallbladder, diverticulum, pancreas, abscess or postoperative collection.Ask whether treatment is antibiotics/supportive care, drainage, interval strategy or operation.
    HemorrhageGI, solid-organ, vascular or postoperative bleeding.Stabilize first; identify when endoscopy, IR, surgery or observation is the definitive path.
    Hernia / closed-loop processPainful irreducible hernia, obstructive features, skin/systemic signs.Differentiate reducible/elective disease from incarceration/strangulation requiring urgent operative evaluation.

    F. ACUTE ABDOMEN - LEARN THE SIX SURGICAL SYNDROMES — G. THE OPERATIVE DECISION RULE

    • Operate / control the source when delay risks irreversible harm - e.g., uncontrolled hemorrhage, perforation/peritonitis, ischemic/strangulated bowel or other immediately correctable surgical catastrophe.
    • Drain / decompress / endoscope / IR when a less invasive source-control route is the appropriate definitive or bridging treatment.
    • Observe / treat nonoperatively only when the patient is stable, the disease supports that strategy and there is a clear reassessment / failure threshold.
    • Never let a test delay treatment when the clinical state already determines the next lifesaving step.
