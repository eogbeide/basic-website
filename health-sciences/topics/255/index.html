CHIEF-COMPLAINT + TOX / EXPOSURE MAP — 7. The first emergency-medicine decision is acuity, not diagnosis DEEP EXPLANATION A patient with a partially recognizable disease can still have an immediate airway, breathing, circulation, neurologic or exposure threat. ED reasoning begins with

    sick/not-sick, monitoring and reversible killers before a complete differential.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Students often feel compelled to name the diagnosis before treating the obvious physiologic danger. EASIEST CLUE Ask: What could kill this patient in the next few minutes, and can I reverse any of it now? — 8. Train by chief complaint and dangerous differential - not by disease chapter DEEP EXPLANATION The ED receives chest pain, dyspnea, abdominal pain, syncope, headache, fever or altered mental status, not a labeled diagnosis. Strong performance comes from knowing

    one common cause, several must-not-miss alternatives and the discriminators that separate them.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Disease-by-disease study feels organized but does not reproduce the uncertainty of an emergency stem. EASIEST CLUE For every complaint, name one common cause + 2-4 dangerous alternatives before looking for the decisive clue. — 9. Toxicology is physiology + ECG / acid-base reasoning + supportive care, not an antidote flashcard contest DEEP EXPLANATION Many overdoses are managed first by airway/ventilation, circulation, temperature control and treatment of seizures or dysrhythmias. Toxidrome, ECG, acid-base pattern,

    timing and agent-specific tests determine when an antidote or elimination strategy belongs.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Learners memorize one-to-one poison-antidote pairs and miss the immediate physiologic threat or a misleading early presentation. EASIEST CLUE Start with vitals + pupils/skin/secretions + ECG + acid-base; then add the specific toxin pathway. — 10. Disposition is part of the diagnosis and management answer DEEP EXPLANATION Emergency care is incomplete until response to treatment is reassessed and the patient is placed in the correct next setting: procedure/OR/cath lab, ICU, ward, observation

    or discharge with feasible follow-up and return precautions.
