    B. CURRENT DELIVERY MODEL + BLUEPRINT VERSION — 1. The Level 2-CE blueprint is a two-dimensional matrix, not two independent study lists DEEP EXPLANATION Dimension 1 describes what physician competency is being tested; Dimension 2 describes the clinical presentation through which it is tested. A chest-pain item may test

    knowledge, patient-care sequencing, communication, professionalism, systems practice or osteopathic integration depending on the task.

    B. CURRENT DELIVERY MODEL + BLUEPRINT VERSION — WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Students often study one list as competencies and another as body systems, then fail to connect them during questions. That creates knowledge without task recognition. EASIEST CLUE For every miss ask two questions: What presentation was this? What competency failed? 2. Blueprint percentages are minimum test specifications - not a literal hour-by-hour study budget DEEP EXPLANATION The percentages define examination representation, but study time should also reflect the learner's own error recurrence, clerkship decay and readiness evidence. A 5% domain that

    is repeatedly missed may deserve more than 5% of remediation time.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Percentages feel objective, so learners may convert them directly into a calendar even when personal weaknesses are highly uneven. EASIEST CLUE Use blueprint weights to protect coverage; use your errors to set intensity. 3. The 30% Patient Care domain is broader than memorizing procedures DEEP EXPLANATION Patient care includes acquiring and interpreting clinical information and choosing appropriate diagnostic, therapeutic, preventive and procedural actions. Many Level 2-CE questions

    therefore hinge on the next correct action rather than disease-name recall.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT The word "procedural" can make students overfocus on technique trivia while undertraining clinical sequencing and management. EASIEST CLUE When a vignette asks what to do next, think Patient Care before pure recall.
