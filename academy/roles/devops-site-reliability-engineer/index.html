Full Role Guide — What This Role Actually Does, Tools, Interview Questions & FAQ

DevOps and SRE are two names for a closely related job: making sure software that works on someone's laptop also works reliably for real users, at 3am, under load, when a dependency goes down. At Google-style SRE and OpenAI-style security/reliability/delivery roles, the job is equal parts building the automation that prevents incidents and being the person who calmly fixes them when prevention wasn't enough.

The mental model that separates this from generic “ops” work is treating reliability as an engineering problem with a budget, not a moral virtue to maximize infinitely. Google popularized the error budget for a reason: 100% uptime is usually the wrong target, because the effort to get from 99.9% to 99.99% often isn't worth what it costs in shipping velocity. A good SRE can argue, with data, when to invest in reliability and when to ship faster instead.

Day to day, that shows up as infrastructure as code so environments are reproducible, CI/CD pipelines with staged or canary rollouts so a bad deploy affects 1% of traffic instead of 100%, and instrumentation good enough that when something breaks, you're reading a dashboard instead of guessing. The mastery path mirrors that arc: automate the infrastructure, build the pipeline, then own the service through a real incident and recovery.

Tools of the Trade

  • Infrastructure as code: Terraform or equivalent
  • CI/CD systems with staged/canary deployment support
  • Kubernetes or another container orchestration platform
  • Observability: metrics, logs, traces, and an alerting strategy that doesn't page you for noise
  • Incident response process: on-call, postmortems, and blameless root-cause analysis

Typical Interview Questions

  • Walk me through how you'd design a canary rollout for a risky deployment.
  • A service's error rate spikes at 2am. Walk me through your triage process.
  • How do you decide when an alert is worth paging someone for, versus a dashboard someone checks in the morning?
  • What's an error budget, and how would you use one to make a real prioritization decision?
  • Describe a postmortem you've written or would write for a real incident — what makes it blameless and useful?
  • How do you approach infrastructure drift, where the real environment stops matching what's in code?

How to Know You’re Ready

  • You've written infrastructure as code that survived being re-run by someone else without your help.
  • You can explain a canary or staged rollout strategy and why it beats an all-at-once deploy.
  • You've been on the other end of a real incident — diagnosed it, or at least understand what good triage looks like under pressure.
  • You have an opinion, backed by a tradeoff, on how much reliability is “enough” for a given service.

FAQ

Is DevOps the same job as SRE?

Closely related but not identical in emphasis. DevOps traditionally emphasizes the culture and tooling that connects development and operations — CI/CD, automation, collaboration. SRE (a term Google coined) applies software-engineering discipline specifically to reliability, often with concepts like error budgets and formal SLOs. Many job postings use the titles interchangeably.

Do I need to know Kubernetes specifically?

It's extremely common at this scale, but the transferable skill is reasoning about distributed systems reliably — orchestration, service discovery, rollout strategy. Don't over-index on one tool at the expense of the underlying judgment.

How much software engineering skill do I need, versus operations skill?

More than the “ops” stereotype suggests. Modern SRE/DevOps roles expect you to write real code — automation, tooling, sometimes services — not just operate infrastructure through a UI. If you can't write and test code, you'll hit a ceiling quickly.

Role-Readiness Proof / Capstone

Own a production service from commit to incident recovery: automate infrastructure with Terraform, build a tested CI/CD pipeline with staged or canary rollout, run the service on Kubernetes, define SLOs and error budgets, instrument metrics/logs/traces, create actionable alerts, execute a failure and incident drill, harden identity and cloud controls, test backup/recovery, and quantify capacity, latency, reliability and cost tradeoffs.

Proof Rubric

  • Show software engineering applied to operations: reproducible IaC
  • trusted build and release gates
  • safe progressive delivery and rollback
  • Kubernetes debugging
  • useful SLOs and alerts
  • observability that shortens diagnosis
  • disciplined incident response and postmortems
  • least privilege and recovery evidence
  • capacity/performance measurements
  • and clear toil-reduction automation tied to reliability outcomes
