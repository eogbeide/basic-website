Full Role Guide — What This Role Actually Does, Tools, Interview Questions & FAQ

Digital Marketing postings at the level Apple's performance media and enterprise growth teams hire for describe a job that's less about creative taste and more about full-funnel accountability: you own a growth number, and every channel — SEO, paid media, lifecycle email, creative — is a lever you're expected to pull and measure, not a silo you specialize in and forget the rest.

The skill that separates a marketer who can run a campaign from one who can run a business-relevant growth program is instrumentation and attribution: knowing what actually drove a conversion, not just what happened right before it. Current tooling (GA4-style analytics, multi-touch attribution) makes this more measurable than it used to be, but also easier to get wrong — a channel that looks like it's underperforming might just be undercredited by whatever attribution model you're using.

The other current-market shift this path weights heavily: AEO — answer engine optimization, being discoverable inside AI-generated search answers, not just traditional SEO rankings — alongside the classic disciplines. A marketer who only thinks in terms of blue-link SEO is increasingly missing where a growing share of discovery actually happens.

Tools of the Trade

  • GA4 or equivalent analytics instrumentation
  • Paid media platforms and campaign optimization
  • SEO and increasingly AEO (answer-engine optimization)
  • Lifecycle/email marketing and audience segmentation
  • A/B testing methodology for creative and channel decisions
  • Attribution modeling — and skepticism about what it's actually telling you

Typical Interview Questions

  • Walk me through how you'd build a full-funnel campaign from positioning through paid media through lifecycle email.
  • How do you decide whether a channel's poor apparent performance is real, or an attribution modeling artifact?
  • Describe an A/B test you ran (or would run) on creative, and how you'd interpret an ambiguous result.
  • How is optimizing for AI-driven answer engines different from traditional SEO?
  • Tell me about a campaign that underperformed. How did you diagnose why?
  • How do you allocate budget across channels when leadership wants growth and finance wants efficiency?

How to Know You’re Ready

  • You can explain a real campaign's results across the full funnel, not just top-of-funnel metrics like impressions or clicks.
  • You've questioned an attribution model's output instead of taking it at face value.
  • You've run an A/B test with a result you didn't expect, and adjusted your approach because of it.
  • You can speak to both traditional SEO and answer-engine/AI discoverability, not just one.

FAQ

Do I need to be a specialist in one channel, or a generalist across all of them?

Most current roles, especially at the level this path benchmarks against, expect full-funnel fluency — you don't need to be the world's best paid-media buyer, but you need to understand how paid, organic, and lifecycle channels interact and be able to reason across all of them.

How important is AEO (answer-engine optimization) right now versus traditional SEO?

It's a genuinely growing share of how people discover things, alongside traditional search — not a replacement for SEO fundamentals, but an increasingly important addition. Marketers who ignore it are optimizing for a shrinking slice of total discovery.

What's the biggest mistake junior digital marketers make?

Optimizing for a metric that's easy to move (impressions, clicks, opens) instead of the one that actually matters to the business (qualified conversions, revenue, retention). It's easy to “win” on a vanity metric while the number leadership cares about doesn't move.
