    EMERGENCY MEDICINE - UNDIFFERENTIATED COMPLAINTS

    ShareXinf

    YEAR 3 | PAGE 32F - DANGEROUS DIFFERENTIALS -> NEXT TEST -> TREATMENT

    -> DISPOSITION

    The emergency physician rarely receives the diagnosis as the chief complaint. Train by complaint, not by disease chapter: stabilize first, identify the cannot-miss alternatives, use a few decisive discriminators, then choose the next test or treatment that changes outcome.

    D. HIGH-YIELD CHIEF-COMPLAINT FRAMEWORK

    ComplaintDangerous alternatives to actively excludeDecision anchors
    Chest painACS, PE, aortic dissection, tension PTX, tamponade, esophageal rupture.Stability first; early ECG; risk factors/tempo; targeted biomarkers/imaging; serial reassessment when disease evolves over time.
    DyspneaSevere asthma/COPD, pulmonary edema/ACS, PE, pneumonia/sepsis, PTX, anaphylaxis, metabolic acidosis.Work of breathing + oxygenation/ventilation + lung/cardiac exam; ECG/CXR/POCUS/ABG-VBG when they will change the next action.
    Abdominal / flank painAAA, mesenteric ischemia, perforation, obstruction, appendicitis, biliary disease, pancreatitis, ectopic/torsion.Peritoneal signs + hemodynamics + age/risk + pregnancy status; choose US vs CT vs immediate surgical pathway by syndrome.
    AMS / syncopeHypoglycemia, stroke/ICH, seizure/postictal, infection, toxidrome, hypoxia/hypercapnia, arrhythmia, hemorrhage.Glucose + vitals + neuro/cardiac clues; medication/exposure history; ECG; selective imaging/LP/toxicology testing based on mechanism.
    Fever / sepsisOccult source, meningitis, necrotizing infection, cholangitis, pyelonephritis, endocarditis; immunocompromised patients may lack fever.Perfusion and organ dysfunction determine urgency; cultures/lactate/source imaging should not delay time-critical treatment in unstable patients.
    Headache / focal neuroSAH/ICH, ischemic stroke, meningitis/encephalitis, venous thrombosis, temporal arteritis, acute glaucoma.Sudden onset, neuro deficit, fever/meningismus, pregnancy/postpartum, immunosuppression and age change the imaging/LP sequence.
    GU/GYN / pelvic painEctopic pregnancy, torsion, PID/TOA, pyelonephritis, obstructing infected stone, testicular torsion.Pregnancy status and time-sensitive organ threat often determine the next imaging/consult step.

    D. HIGH-YIELD CHIEF-COMPLAINT FRAMEWORK — E. NEXT-TEST LOGIC: AVOID THE THREE CLASSIC EM ERRORS

    • Error 1 - confirming before stabilizing: the definitive study is not the next step when the patient first needs airway, decompression, hemorrhage control, glucose, anticonvulsant therapy or another lifesaving intervention.
    • Error 2 - overtesting a low-risk patient: an ED workup is not “everything.” Use pretest probability, decision rules and red flags only in populations where they are validated.
    • Error 3 - stopping after the first negative test: evolving ACS, sepsis, abdominal disease and neurologic emergencies may require serial assessment, repeat testing, observation or escalation.

    F. DISPOSITION IS PART OF THE DIAGNOSIS

    DispositionWhat you must be able to defend
    ICU / resuscitation areaOngoing organ support, shock, unstable airway/ventilation, malignant rhythm, active neurologic deterioration or need for very frequent reassessment.
    OR / cath lab / procedural pathwayTime-dependent source control, hemorrhage, ischemia or mechanical catastrophe where definitive intervention outranks further diagnostic delay.
    Admission / observationUnresolved risk, need for serial biomarkers/exams, IV therapy, oxygen, inability to tolerate PO, unsafe home context or diagnostic uncertainty requiring monitoring.
    DischargeDangerous diagnoses reasonably excluded/controlled; vitals/functional status acceptable; treatment and follow-up feasible; explicit return precautions understood.
