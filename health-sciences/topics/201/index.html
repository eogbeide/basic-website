    B. TOP 10 MOST-MISSED SYSTEM CONCEPTS - #1 TO #3

    # / CONCEPTDEEP EXPLANATIONWHY STUDENTS MISS ITEASIEST CLUE
    1. Anemia starts with marrow responseMCV is useful, but the reticulocyte response tells you whether marrow output is appropriate. Low/insufficient retics suggest underproduction; an appropriate rise points toward blood loss or destruction, after correcting for the degree of anemia.Students memorize micro/normo/macrocytic lists and skip the production-vs-loss decision.Ask is the marrow responding? before chasing the MCV differential.
    2. Hemolysis must be localized, not just recognizedHemolysis raises LDH and indirect bilirubin and lowers haptoglobin; smear and clinical context then separate intravascular, splenic/extravascular and microangiopathic processes.The same generic “hemolysis labs” appear in many disorders, so learners stop before identifying the mechanism.Use smear + urine/plasma hemoglobin + spleen/Coombs context to localize destruction.
    3. Bleeding phenotype comes before the coagulation cascadeMucocutaneous bleeding suggests platelet/vWF problems; deep tissue/hemarthrosis suggests factor deficiency. Then platelet count, PT/aPTT, fibrinogen and mixing studies identify the failing arm and whether an inhibitor is present.Students start by drawing the cascade and miss the clinical split between primary and secondary hemostasis.First ask platelet-type or factor-type bleeding?
