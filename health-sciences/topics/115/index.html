    E. PEDIATRIC EMERGENCY FIRST-PASS

    ThreatImmediate questionsHigh-yield next-step logic
    Respiratory failureUpper airway obstruction, lower airway disease, parenchymal disease or central/neuromuscular problem? Is fatigue developing?Support oxygenation/ventilation appropriate to severity while treating the cause; worsening effort with declining mental status is a danger sign.
    Shock / sepsisPerfusion, mental status, pulses, capillary refill, urine output, fever/infection clues and source?Recognize compensated shock before hypotension; obtain access, targeted resuscitation, early antimicrobials/source control when infection is suspected, then reassess.
    Seizure / statusStill seizing? glucose issue? fever? focality? trauma/toxin? meningitis/encephalitis concern?ABCs and glucose first; stop ongoing seizure, then determine etiology and need for imaging, LP, admission or specialist escalation.
    DKA / metabolic crisisPolyuria/polydipsia, weight loss, dehydration, tachypnea, altered mental status, glucose/ketone/acidosis pattern?Fluids, insulin/electrolyte strategy and neurologic monitoring are pediatric-specific; avoid treating the glucose number in isolation.
    AnaphylaxisRapid multisystem allergic syndrome with airway, breathing or circulatory involvement?Immediate epinephrine pathway and supportive care; do not substitute antihistamines for first-line treatment.
    Trauma / maltreatmentDoes mechanism fit development and injury pattern? Any head/abdominal injury or unsafe disposition?Stabilize trauma first; preserve a broad differential and follow mandatory reporting / child-protection processes when concern exists.

    F. DEHYDRATION + FLUID REASONING - DO NOT SKIP THE CLINICAL ESTIMATE

    QuestionWhat to integrate
    How sick?Mental status, perfusion, mucous membranes/tears, urine output, respiratory pattern, vital signs and weight change when available.
    Which route?Oral/enteral rehydration when the child can safely tolerate it; IV resuscitation for shock/severe dehydration or failed enteral strategy.
    What fluid problem?Resuscitation, maintenance and replacement are different jobs. Track ongoing GI/renal losses and electrolyte abnormalities separately.
    What changed after treatment?Repeat perfusion, heart rate, work of breathing, urine output, intake and exam. Response is new diagnostic information.

    F. DEHYDRATION + FLUID REASONING - DO NOT SKIP THE CLINICAL ESTIMATE — G. SAFEGUARDING + COMMUNICATION ARE EXAM CONTENT

    • Interview the child and caregivers in an age-appropriate way; use professional interpreters when needed and respect adolescent confidentiality within safety limits.
    • Injury should fit the stated mechanism and the child's developmental capability. Delay in care, inconsistent history or unusual injury patterns raise concern.
    • When abuse/neglect is suspected, the medical student's job is to recognize concern, protect the child, document objectively and escalate through the supervising team and required reporting process.
