    YEAR 3 | PAGE 30A - AGE-SPECIFIC NORMAL -> ABNORMAL -> PREVENTION

    WHY PEDIATRICS REQUIRES A DIFFERENT MENTAL MODEL In pediatrics, age changes the differential, normal vital signs, developmental expectations, medication/fluids, communication and disposition. Start every case with age and developmental context. A child is not a small adult: the same symptom can represent a benign developmental stage in one age group and a time-critical disease in another.

    A. THE PEDIATRIC FIRST-PASS FRAME

    StepAsk yourselfWhy it matters
    1. Age + settingNewborn, infant, toddler, school-age or adolescent? Well visit, clinic, ED or inpatient?Immediately reshapes normal ranges, likely diagnoses, communication and urgency.
    2. Growth + developmentIs growth tracking? Are milestones being gained on time? Any regression or school/behavior concern?Trend and trajectory are more useful than one isolated number. Regression is different from delayed acquisition.
    3. Prevention + contextVaccines, screening, nutrition, sleep, injury prevention, psychosocial environment and safeguarding?Pediatrics tests prevention and anticipatory guidance as part of routine care, not as an afterthought.
    4. SeverityWork of breathing, perfusion, hydration, mental status, fever-risk age, pain and toxicity?A stable child allows stepwise evaluation; an unstable child needs age-appropriate stabilization first.
    5. Weight-based decisionsWhat is the current weight and which treatment depends on kg?Fluids and many medications are weight based; estimate only when true weight is unavailable in an emergency.
    6. Family + dispositionCan caregivers recognize worsening, give treatment safely and access follow-up?Safe discharge depends on the child plus caregiver capacity, return precautions and follow-up.

    B. NEWBORN / WELL-CHILD HIGH-YIELD CHECK

    DomainReasoning anchor
    Transition + newborn examLook for respiratory/circulatory instability, congenital anomalies, feeding problems and findings that need immediate evaluation versus routine follow-up.
    Feeding + weightInterpret feeding effectiveness, output and weight trajectory together; distinguish expected early change from dehydration, poor intake or disease.
    JaundiceAge at onset, bilirubin pattern, gestational/risk context and illness signs determine whether this is physiologic-pattern jaundice or pathologic disease requiring workup/treatment.
    Growth + developmentPlot trends; screen development at recommended intervals; ask whether skills are being acquired, lost or uneven across motor/language/social domains.
    PreventionUse the current official immunization schedule and age-specific screening/anticipatory guidance rather than memorizing an outdated vaccine calendar.
    SafeguardingAsk whether injury/history fits developmental ability. Unexplained injury in a nonmobile child or inconsistent history requires careful escalation and supervised evaluation.
