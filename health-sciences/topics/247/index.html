    E. TRIANGULATE THE SIGNALS — 7. Different performance channels diagnose different problems DEEP EXPLANATION A strong Qbank does not prove strong bedside synthesis, and positive clinical feedback does not guarantee complete subject-exam coverage. Combining the channels prevents one strength from masking another

    weakness.

    E. TRIANGULATE THE SIGNALS — WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Students naturally privilege the metric they can quantify most easily, usually question percentage. EASIEST CLUE For every mid-rotation weakness, label the channel: knowledge, reasoning, communication, clinical execution, professionalism/workflow, or coverage. 8. A weak chief complaint is usually a sequence problem, not a disease-list problem DEEP EXPLANATION When chest pain, abdominal pain, dyspnea, altered mental status or another presentation remains unstable, rebuild it as an algorithm: stabilize if needed -> localize syndrome -> dangerous differential -> first test ->

    confirmatory test -> initial treatment -> disposition.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Disease lists create recognition but do not specify what happens first, what can wait, or what sends the patient home versus to the ICU/OR. EASIEST CLUE If you keep asking what is the diagnosis? but the exam asks what next?, switch to an action sequence. 9. Feedback only works when it becomes a measurable next behavior DEEP EXPLANATION "Be more concise" or "improve your differential" is too vague to drive change. Translate feedback into a specific next-shift behavior, practice it, and seek confirmation that the behavior changed. This closes the feedback

    loop.
