WEAK-AREA PRESCRIPTION MATRIX — E. 20-MINUTE PATIENT-TO-BOARD BRIDGE

    0-3 min Compress the patient into one sentence: age/context + syndrome + tempo + key discriminators + severity. 3-6 min Generate must-not-miss + most likely + closest mimic; identify what data would separate them. 6-10 min State the next test/treatment step and why it changes management; include stability and disposition. 10-15 min Use one targeted source only for the unresolved gap; stop when the named uncertainty is answered. 15-20 min Close resources; retrieve the algorithm from memory; create one delayed new-question target.

WEAK-AREA PRESCRIPTION MATRIX — 7. A missed question should be classified before a resource is chosen DEEP EXPLANATION The same wrong answer can arise from different causes: missing disease knowledge, failure to compress the stem, wrong physician-task identification, poor diagnostic-threshold

    judgment, management-sequence confusion or unfamiliar exam findings. Each cause requires a different repair tool.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT Students often respond to every miss by rereading the disease article. That may add facts while leaving the original reasoning failure untouched. EASIEST CLUE Name the failure in one phrase: knowledge, representation, task, threshold, sequence, timing or confidence - then prescribe. 8. Bedside and case resources should change clinical reasoning, not merely add visual familiarity DEEP EXPLANATION Physical-exam videos and case resources are most useful when connected to a prediction: what finding do I expect, how would it alter the differential, and what decision would

    change? Hands-on maneuvers remain supervised clinical skills rather than self-certified video competencies.

    WHY STUDENTS MISS IT It is easy to watch a polished demonstration and feel fluent without being able to perform, interpret or integrate the finding into management. EASIEST CLUE For every bedside resource ask what finding, what likelihood shift, what management consequence? F.
