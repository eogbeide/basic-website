    YEAR 3 | PAGE 63D - 10 HIGH-DISCRIMINATION CASES + CURRENT NBME / COMAT TRANSFER

    FINAL REVIEW SHOULD FORCE THE FIRST SAFE DECISION: AGE / DEVELOPMENT OR PREGNANCY / GESTATIONAL AGE -> STABILITY -> SYNDROME -> DISCRIMINATOR -> AGE- OR PREGNANCY-SPECIFIC NEXT STEP -> REASSESSMENT / DISPOSITION. G. 10 HIGH-DISCRIMINATION SHELF / COMAT CASES

    #Case patternBest exam reasoning
    1Newborn develops jaundice very early with poor feeding / illness signs.Do not label physiologic jaundice from color alone; timing, illness and bilirubin pattern determine pathologic evaluation.
    2Infant bronchiolitis with increasing work of breathing and declining interaction.Severity/trajectory outrank the diagnostic label; support oxygenation/ventilation and escalate when fatigue suggests impending failure.
    3Young infant with fever and toxic appearance.Age + appearance lower the threshold for serious bacterial infection evaluation and supervised stabilization; adult fever logic is unsafe.
    4Child with episodic abdominal pain, vomiting and concerning stool findings.Think age-specific obstruction/intussusception pattern rather than default gastroenteritis; choose the targeted imaging/procedural pathway.
    5Child with dehydration but preserved perfusion and ability to drink.Use clinical severity to choose oral/enteral rehydration when appropriate; IV resuscitation is for shock/severe dehydration or failed enteral strategy.
    6First-trimester pain/bleeding with pregnancy of unknown location.Localize pregnancy and assess stability; ectopic pregnancy remains a must-not-miss until location/trajectory clarify the diagnosis.
    7Pregnant patient with seizure + severe hypertension / end-organ features.Maternal stabilization, seizure treatment/prevention and severe-BP control come before complete diagnostic refinement; then determine delivery timing.
    8Third-trimester painless bleeding before placental location is known.Stabilize and use ultrasound/obstetric evaluation; avoid unsafe digital cervical examination when placenta previa is possible.
    9Postpartum heavy bleeding with boggy uterus.Resuscitate while using the 4 Ts; uterine atony (Tone) is a leading mechanism and requires rapid cause-directed escalation.
    10Sudden unilateral pelvic pain + adnexal mass in a stable reproductive-age patient.Establish pregnancy status and treat torsion as a time-sensitive gynecologic emergency; do not delay for a chronic pelvic-pain workup.
