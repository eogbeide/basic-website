    B. TOP 10 MOST-MISSED SYSTEM CONCEPTS - #1 TO #4

    # / CONCEPTDEEP EXPLANATIONWHY STUDENTS MISS ITEASIEST CLUE
    1. Preload vs afterload vs contractilityPreload is end-diastolic filling/stretch; afterload is the pressure/resistance the ventricle must eject against; contractility is intrinsic squeeze at a given preload/afterload. PV loops encode all three.Students memorize arrow tables without anchoring each variable to a physical event, so one novel valve/drug perturbation breaks the pattern.Ask: fill, push against, or squeeze?
    2. Murmur timing is pressure logicA murmur occurs when turbulent flow crosses a valve during the phase in which that valve should be open or is pathologically leaking. Timing + location + radiation + maneuvers are downstream of hemodynamics.Murmurs are often learned as sound-label pairs, so changes with preload/afterload seem arbitrary.First decide systole or diastole, then ask which valve has abnormal flow in that phase.
    3. ECG is conduction, not a picture dictionaryRate, rhythm, P-QRS-T timing, intervals, axis and ST-T changes reflect depolarization/repolarization across tissue. Electrolytes, ischemia and drugs alter the underlying electrical process.Pattern recognition is fast but brittle; students may name a tracing yet fail to explain the altered tissue/conduction step.Translate every abnormality into where conduction or repolarization changed.
    4. Shock profiles come from pump, volume and resistanceCardiogenic, hypovolemic/distributive and obstructive states differ in preload, pump function, vascular tone and compensatory physiology. The same low BP can arise from different causal chains.Learners anchor on hypotension alone and miss the hemodynamic pattern.Think tank - pump - pipes - obstruction.
